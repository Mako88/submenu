Sweep 022 -- what is the +0.197 that homeostasis alone buys?
============================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
An earlier measurement turned up something nobody went looking for: a network
with **every learning rule switched off** still gets substantially better over
its first fifty rounds. More than twice as much as the clever learning mechanism
adds on top. Nothing in this project explains it.

Only three things adjust themselves in that condition, so this switches each off
in turn to see which one matters.

The trick is that simply switching off the main one kills the network -- it goes
silent, and a silent network scores badly for reasons that have nothing to do
with the question. So there's a fairer version: let a twin network find the right
settings, copy them across, *then* switch the adjusting off. That separates
"finding the right setting" from "continuously readjusting".

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 019 surfaced it as a side effect and nobody asked. A column with `lr=0`,
no binding and no lateral inhibition -- no learning rule of any kind -- climbs
from 0.582 to 0.779 linear decodability over its first fifty episodes and then
plateaus. That is **+0.197, more than twice what salience-gated Hebbian binding
adds on top of it**, and this project has no account of it.

It matters beyond curiosity. Every mechanism measured here is measured as a
delta on top of that settling, so if the settling is most of the effect, effort
spent on rules layered above it is being spent in the wrong place. And if the
state it arrives at can be reached without the fifty episodes, that is a
straightforward win available today.

Exactly three things adapt in that condition:

  somatic threshold   theta, multiplicative, targeting `target_rate`
  dendritic knee      per-branch, targeting `plateau_engagement`
  synaptic scaling    per-branch renormalisation of W to `branch_budget`

So the question is an ablation, not a hypothesis.

WHY THE OBVIOUS ABLATION DOES NOT ANSWER IT
--------------------------------------------
Measured at one seed, 20 episodes, before designing the rest of this sweep:

  condition        decodability@20   sparsity
  all                    0.800         0.029
  no-knee                0.600         0.029
  no-scaling             0.633         0.028
  no-theta               0.567         0.004
  none                   0.567         0.002

The bottom two rows are **not results**. Without threshold adaptation the column
falls to a fifth of its target firing rate and is effectively silent, and a
silent column decodes at its starting value for reasons that have nothing to do
with the mechanism under test. Reporting "threshold homeostasis contributes
nothing" from that row would be a conclusion drawn from a disconnected quantity
-- the exact failure this project is built against.

The sparsity column is in the probe for this reason and is printed next to
decodability rather than on request.

THE CONTROL THAT DOES ANSWER IT
--------------------------------
Separate the two things homeostasis does: it *finds* an operating point, and it
*keeps* adapting. `--preset-from N` settles a twin column -- same seed, so the
same weights and the same wiring -- for N episodes with everything adapting,
copies its `theta` and `knee` onto the column being measured, and only then
switches adaptation off. Sparsity is preserved and what is removed is the
ongoing adaptation.

One seed, `--preset-from 50`:

  condition        decodability@0    @20    sparsity@20
  preset-frozen          0.767      0.767      0.020
  theta-preset           0.767      0.733      0.028

Against `all` starting at 0.567 and reaching 0.800 after 20 episodes.

**At episode 0, with no adaptation running at all, a column handed the converged
threshold and knee is already at 0.767.** The +0.197 looks like it is the
operating point rather than the settling process -- homeostasis is not learning
anything, it is locating the values at which a pre-existing random column is
readable.

PREDICTION (recorded before the 20-seed run)
---------------------------------------------
  all              0.582 -> 0.780 by episode 50, flat after   (replicates 019)
  preset-frozen    0.76 to 0.80 at episode 0, and FLAT throughout. This is the
                   sharp one. If ongoing adaptation matters, this curve must
                   climb; if the operating point is the whole story, it does not
                   move at all.
  theta-preset     within 0.02 of preset-frozen. Knee and scaling contribute
                   little once theta is right.
  no-knee          0.60 to 0.68, sparsity healthy at 0.025 to 0.032
  no-scaling       0.62 to 0.70, sparsity healthy
  no-theta         flat at its episode-0 value, sparsity below 0.008 --
                   recorded as an uninterpretable condition rather than a null,
                   and included precisely so that is on the record

Stated so it can be wrong: **if `preset-frozen` starts high and stays flat, the
+0.197 is a per-neuron operating point -- a few hundred scalars -- and not a
learned trajectory.** The fifty episodes are search, not accumulation.

CONSEQUENCE, NAMED IN ADVANCE
------------------------------
If that holds, the next question is immediate and cheap: does the twin need
*task* input to find those values, or would any input with matching statistics
do? If random input suffices, the operating point is precomputable and fifty
episodes of every future run are free. That is sweep 023 and it should not be
folded into this one.

If instead `preset-frozen` climbs, then ongoing adaptation is doing real work
across training and the +0.197 is a genuine unsupervised learning effect -- in
which case it is the largest one in the project and the mechanisms layered above
it deserve re-examination against it rather than against a frozen baseline.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
--------------------------
The preset column has had fifty episodes of unsupervised exposure through its
twin, so "free" is the wrong word until sweep 023 runs. What is being claimed is
narrower and still substantial: that the thing carried forward is a small set of
per-neuron scalars rather than a trajectory through weight space. Nothing here
touches labels -- the twin sees no error signal, and `lr=0` throughout.

Absolute levels will sit below sweep 019's because the probe uses fewer
collection episodes. Compare shapes and gaps across conditions, not levels
against 019.

THE OPERATING POINT IS THE ANSWER
----------------------------------
`preset-frozen` starts at **0.752 against `all`'s 0.582**, with threshold, knee
and scaling adaptation all disabled and `lr=0`. Nothing in that column is
learning anything. Handing it a settled twin's threshold and knee accounts for
**+0.170 of the +0.197**, or 86% of the way to where `all` plateaus (~0.76).

So the answer to "what is the +0.197?" is: **a few hundred per-neuron scalars.**
It is a search for an operating point, not an accumulation. Fifty episodes of
unsupervised settling produce a state that could have been handed over directly.

`theta-preset` -- preset, threshold adaptation off, knee and scaling still
running -- sits at 0.752-0.781 with no trend, within the predicted 0.02 of
`preset-frozen`. Ongoing adaptation on top of a correct operating point adds
nothing detectable.

A FLAW IN MY OWN CONDITION, RECORDED RATHER THAN GLOSSED
---------------------------------------------------------
The prediction called `preset-frozen` "FLAT throughout" and said "if ongoing
adaptation matters, this curve must climb". **That half was untestable by
construction.** With `lr=0`, no binding, no lateral, and all three adaptations
disabled, nothing in the column changes between checkpoints -- and the probe
uses a fixed seed per checkpoint, so the curve is *identical* by arithmetic, not
flat by measurement. It reads 0.752 at all eleven checkpoints to three decimals
because it is literally the same column being measured eleven times.

A condition that cannot fail its own prediction is worth nothing as evidence,
and rule 9 applies to experimental conditions and not only to unit tests. What
carries the finding is the *level* at episode 0 -- 0.752 against 0.582 -- which
is a real comparison between two columns differing only in theta and knee.
`theta-preset` is the condition that could have climbed and did not, so it does
the work the prediction assigned to `preset-frozen`.

Naming it because the same mistake is easy to repeat: check that each condition
has a reachable outcome that would refute the prediction, not just a plausible
one that would confirm it.

SYNAPTIC SCALING IS NOT HELPING, AND THAT WAS NOT PREDICTED
------------------------------------------------------------
`no-scaling` was predicted at 0.62-0.70. It reaches **0.780, above `all`'s
0.755**, with sparsity indistinguishable from the full condition (0.0309 in
both). Removing synaptic scaling costs nothing and may buy a little.

That lands on a decision AUDIT.md already flags. `scaling_lr = 2e-2` was chosen
while synaptic scaling was *dynamically inert* -- it only ever moved a `W` that
the forward pass ignored -- and has never been tuned against a working forward
pass. This is the first measurement of what it contributes, and the answer is
nothing detectable in decodability at 20 seeds. It is not a null hypothesis test
between them, so the honest statement is: **scaling is not carrying the settling
gain, and its live default now has evidence against it rather than none.**

`no-knee` reaches ~0.70 against `all`'s ~0.76 with healthy sparsity (0.0306), so
knee adaptation is worth roughly +0.06 -- predicted 0.60-0.68, slightly under.

THE DEAD-COLUMN CONDITION, AS PREDICTED
-----------------------------------------
`no-theta` sits at 0.577-0.598 throughout with sparsity at 0.0044 against a
target of 0.03. The column is at a seventh of its target rate. Its flat curve is
a fact about a silent column and says nothing about threshold homeostasis, and
it is recorded here so nobody re-derives it. This is why the sparsity table is
printed beside the decodability table rather than on request.

A DETAIL NOBODY ASKED FOR: THE OVERSHOOT
------------------------------------------
`all` reaches **0.813 at episode 10** and then falls back to ~0.76 and stays
there. `no-scaling` does the same, more strongly: 0.834 at episode 10 against a
0.78 plateau. Sweep 019 could not see this -- it checkpointed every 25 episodes.

So settling is not monotone: it overshoots by ~+0.05 and relaxes. Unexplained,
and not chased here, but recorded because "converges to a plateau" is the
natural description and it is wrong.

Also worth noting: `preset-frozen` runs at sparsity 0.0228 against `all`'s
0.0309. The preset operating point is captured from a twin at one moment and
then held fixed, so it sits slightly off what the measured run's own statistics
would settle to. That is a plausible reason 0.752 falls a little short of 0.76,
and it means the preset is a lower bound on what a correct operating point buys.

WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT ESTABLISH
--------------------------------------
Established: the settling gain is an operating point, reproducible by copying
two per-neuron vectors, and ongoing adaptation on top of a correct one adds
nothing measurable over 100 episodes.

Not established: that the operating point is *free*. The twin had fifty episodes
of exposure to the task to find it. No labels and `lr=0` throughout, so it is
unsupervised, but it is still derived from data.

That is sweep 023, exactly as this sweep's prediction named it in advance: does
the twin need *task* input, or would any input with matching statistics do? If
random input suffices, the operating point is precomputable and fifty episodes
of every future run are free. It is a two-condition sweep and should not be
folded into anything else.
