Sweep 006 -- magnitude updates, 24 neurons, 20 seeds, with elig_rms fixed.

Every sweep so far ran with a mis-scaled update. elig_rms normalises the weight
change by the trace's own magnitude, and it was seeded at 1e-3 with a 0.999
decay. That was harmless while the modulator fired ~70 times per episode, but
the "one decision, one release" fix cut it to one update per episode, after
which the EMA advanced 70x more slowly and stayed dominated by its own
initialisation: tracking 4.6e-3 against an actual 2.4e-2 after 200 episodes.

So the effective learning rate ran about 5x high and drifted upward as the
average slowly caught up. Bias correction plus a window measured in updates
brings the tracked value to within 1.17x of the true RMS.

This re-establishes the baseline comparison (equivalent to sweep 002) with the
scaling correct. Sweep 005, which tested sign-only updates, ran before the fix
and is confounded; it should be repeated after this.

Prediction, recorded before the result: the gradient check is independent of
this bug and still says the trace carries no magnitude information, so correct
scaling alone should not rescue the rule. But a 5x drifting learning rate is
enough to mask a small effect either way, and no previous number is clean.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds, ELIG_MODE=magnitude confirmed in the run env:
  frozen 0.592 +/- 0.059, plastic 0.597 +/- 0.066, mean paired difference
  +0.006, better on 8/20, permutation p = 0.66.
  The prediction held: correct scaling alone does not rescue the rule. The
  gradient check's verdict stands, and the baseline is now clean.
