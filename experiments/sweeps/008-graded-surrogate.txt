Sweep 008 -- graded sensitivity, 24 neurons, 20 seeds, magnitude updates.

The first change that improved the measured defect rather than working around
it. Sweeps 001-007 established that the rule does nothing and that the cause is
the eligibility trace: it agreed with the finite-difference gradient in sign
0.64 of the time and in magnitude not at all (correlation -0.045).

The surrogate was the suspect. The standard window, a smooth bump around
threshold, ignores whether the neuron fired at all. But valued events make the
emission genuinely smooth above threshold, so d(value)/du = 1 - tanh^2(u/span)
is a real derivative there, not a surrogate, and exactly zero below. Measured
against finite differences over 90 synapses:

  window   correlation -0.045   sign 0.64 (p 0.0040)   ratio 0.0023
  graded   correlation +0.207   sign 0.69 (p 0.0002)   ratio 0.0773
  hybrid   correlation -0.026   sign 0.63 (p 0.0074)   ratio 0.0078

Graded is better on every axis, and the first positive magnitude correlation in
the project. That hybrid is no better than window is itself informative: adding
the window term back reintroduces the noise, so the window is the problem
rather than an imperfect approximation of the answer.

The forward pass is untouched by this -- the surrogate only enters the
eligibility trace -- so dynamics, sparsity and every frozen-column result are
unaffected.

Prediction, recorded before the result: a correlation of +0.207 is a real
improvement on -0.045 but still far from tracking the gradient properly, so a
detectable effect is plausible but a large one is not. Compare against sweep
006, the clean baseline at these settings: +0.006, p = 0.66.
