Sweep 020 -- lateral inhibition, and whether decorrelation was ever the problem
==============================================================================

QUESTION
--------
Lateral inhibition has been the top build item since sweep 011, on a chain of
reasoning: the RLS readout gains +0.032 by carrying an inverse correlation
matrix; sweep 011 showed that gain does not decompose into per-column pieces,
so it costs the locality rule; sweep 019 showed the readout rather than the
mechanism is the bottleneck. Decorrelating *inside* the column, locally, should
therefore let a cheap readout extract what RLS extracts by pooling.

The mechanism built for it is the Vogels-Sprekeler rule (Vogels et al. 2011):
anti-Hebbian plasticity on the inhibitory synapses that already exist, so that
units firing together strengthen the inhibition between them. Every quantity is
one synapse's own presynaptic trace and its own postsynaptic unit, and the
`- target_rate` term makes it self-limiting rather than runaway.

It is also worth stating plainly that this sweep exists to check a premise, not
to tune a mechanism. Nothing has ever measured whether the representation is
*redundant* in the first place.

ONE SEED, WHICH IS WHY THIS SWEEP EXISTS
-----------------------------------------
Measured in the space decodability actually lives in -- the answer-time readout
trace, not per-step output -- at seed 0 only:

  condition   |corr|   eff.rank   linear    MLP
  off          0.183    16.1/96    0.775   0.975
  lateral      0.172    17.0/96    0.792   0.983
  hebbian      0.255     9.3/96    0.933   0.992

Lateral inhibition does decorrelate, mildly, and buys +0.017 -- within noise at
one seed. Salience-gated Hebbian binding does the *opposite*: correlation rises
from 0.183 to 0.255 and the effective rank nearly halves, from 16.1 to 9.3 of
96. And it is binding that gains +0.158.

If that holds, the premise behind this entire direction is backwards. Binding
does not decorrelate; it **consolidates** -- collapsing the state onto fewer,
more strongly co-varying directions, and that is what makes it linearly
readable. Decorrelation would undo it.

PREDICTION (recorded before the 20-seed run)
---------------------------------------------
High confidence, because the one-seed effect is large and structural rather
than marginal:

  on vs off        corr UP by +0.05 to +0.09, eff_rank DOWN by 5 to 8,
                   linear up by ~+0.07 (already established at +0.074)
  lateral vs off   corr down slightly, eff_rank up slightly, linear null
                   (predicted |diff| < 0.02, p > 0.05)
  lateral+on vs on linear NEGATIVE or null. If consolidation is the mechanism,
                   adding a rule that fights it should not help and may hurt.

The third is the one that decides the direction. Naming the consequence in
advance: **if lateral is null against off and does not improve on binding, the
lateral-inhibition item comes off the TODO and the decorrelation reasoning
behind it is recorded as refuted** -- not parked, not "needs tuning". A premise
that survives its own disconfirming measurement by acquiring hyperparameters is
how a project spends a month on nothing.

What would save it: lateral+on beating on. That would mean the two mechanisms
are complementary -- consolidate the signal, decorrelate the residue -- and the
reasoning was right about the destination while wrong about the route.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
--------------------------
Whether *some* decorrelating mechanism would help. This tests one rule at one
rate. But the correlation and effective-rank columns are the useful part
regardless of which condition wins: they say what shape the representation has,
which nothing in this project has measured before, and they are what any future
mechanism should be aimed at rather than at decodability alone.

A CALIBRATION BUG, FOUND AND FIXED BEFORE THE RUN
--------------------------------------------------
The rule was disconnected on first implementation -- not absent, but four
orders of magnitude too weak. `pre` sits around 0.016, so the raw product moved
inhibitory weights by 2.2e-5 against a weight scale of 0.375. Normalised
against the neuron's own mean presynaptic magnitude, `lateral_lr` now means a
fraction of the weight scale and the weights move by 26% at the default rate.

Identical in form to the eligibility-normaliser bug, where the three-factor
rule moved weights by 0.1% while synaptic scaling moved them by 21% and the
rule was measurably decorative. A test asserts on the *size* of the change
rather than its existence, since a bare connection test passes on 2.2e-5.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, 500 collection episodes, column lr 0.
Four conditions: off, on, lateral, lateral+on.
