Sweep 020 -- lateral inhibition, and whether decorrelation was ever the problem
==============================================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Two earlier findings suggested the network's neurons were too alike -- all
saying similar things -- and that spreading them out would help.

Before building on that assumption, this checks it. It measures how similar the
neurons actually are, and how many genuinely independent things they collectively
represent, under each mechanism.

If the mechanism that *works* turns out to make neurons *more* alike rather than
less, then the reasoning behind the whole direction is backwards, and it's much
better to find that out now than after a month of building on it.

QUESTION
--------
Lateral inhibition has been the top build item since sweep 011, on a chain of
reasoning: the RLS readout gains +0.032 by carrying an inverse correlation
matrix; sweep 011 showed that gain does not decompose into per-column pieces,
so it costs the locality rule; sweep 019 showed the readout rather than the
mechanism is the bottleneck. Decorrelating *inside* the column, locally, should
therefore let a cheap readout extract what RLS extracts by pooling.

The mechanism built for it is the Vogels-Sprekeler rule (Vogels et al. 2011):
anti-Hebbian plasticity on the inhibitory synapses that already exist, so that
units firing together strengthen the inhibition between them. Every quantity is
one synapse's own presynaptic trace and its own postsynaptic unit, and the
`- target_rate` term makes it self-limiting rather than runaway.

It is also worth stating plainly that this sweep exists to check a premise, not
to tune a mechanism. Nothing has ever measured whether the representation is
*redundant* in the first place.

ONE SEED, WHICH IS WHY THIS SWEEP EXISTS
-----------------------------------------
Measured in the space decodability actually lives in -- the answer-time readout
trace, not per-step output -- at seed 0 only:

  condition   |corr|   eff.rank   linear    MLP
  off          0.183    16.1/96    0.775   0.975
  lateral      0.172    17.0/96    0.792   0.983
  hebbian      0.255     9.3/96    0.933   0.992

Lateral inhibition does decorrelate, mildly, and buys +0.017 -- within noise at
one seed. Salience-gated Hebbian binding does the *opposite*: correlation rises
from 0.183 to 0.255 and the effective rank nearly halves, from 16.1 to 9.3 of
96. And it is binding that gains +0.158.

If that holds, the premise behind this entire direction is backwards. Binding
does not decorrelate; it **consolidates** -- collapsing the state onto fewer,
more strongly co-varying directions, and that is what makes it linearly
readable. Decorrelation would undo it.

PREDICTION (recorded before the 20-seed run)
---------------------------------------------
High confidence, because the one-seed effect is large and structural rather
than marginal:

  on vs off        corr UP by +0.05 to +0.09, eff_rank DOWN by 5 to 8,
                   linear up by ~+0.07 (already established at +0.074)
  lateral vs off   corr down slightly, eff_rank up slightly, linear null
                   (predicted |diff| < 0.02, p > 0.05)
  lateral+on vs on linear NEGATIVE or null. If consolidation is the mechanism,
                   adding a rule that fights it should not help and may hurt.

The third is the one that decides the direction. Naming the consequence in
advance: **if lateral is null against off and does not improve on binding, the
lateral-inhibition item comes off the TODO and the decorrelation reasoning
behind it is recorded as refuted** -- not parked, not "needs tuning". A premise
that survives its own disconfirming measurement by acquiring hyperparameters is
how a project spends a month on nothing.

What would save it: lateral+on beating on. That would mean the two mechanisms
are complementary -- consolidate the signal, decorrelate the residue -- and the
reasoning was right about the destination while wrong about the route.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT SETTLE
--------------------------
Whether *some* decorrelating mechanism would help. This tests one rule at one
rate. But the correlation and effective-rank columns are the useful part
regardless of which condition wins: they say what shape the representation has,
which nothing in this project has measured before, and they are what any future
mechanism should be aimed at rather than at decodability alone.

A CALIBRATION BUG, FOUND AND FIXED BEFORE THE RUN
--------------------------------------------------
The rule was disconnected on first implementation -- not absent, but four
orders of magnitude too weak. `pre` sits around 0.016, so the raw product moved
inhibitory weights by 2.2e-5 against a weight scale of 0.375. Normalised
against the neuron's own mean presynaptic magnitude, `lateral_lr` now means a
fraction of the weight scale and the weights move by 26% at the default rate.

Identical in form to the eligibility-normaliser bug, where the three-factor
rule moved weights by 0.1% while synaptic scaling moved them by 21% and the
rule was measurably decorative. A test asserts on the *size* of the change
rather than its existence, since a bare connection test passes on 2.2e-5.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, 500 collection episodes, column lr 0.
Four conditions: off, on, lateral, lateral+on.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds
-------------------------
condition        linear     mlp     |corr|   eff_rank   sparsity
off               0.802   0.987      0.178     17.404      0.031
lateral           0.830   0.990      0.175     17.139      0.031
on                0.876   0.999      0.255      9.641      0.034
lateral+on        0.913   1.000      0.256      9.383      0.034

  on vs off          linear   +0.074   18/20   p = 0.0000
                     corr     +0.077   20/20   p = 0.0000
                     eff_rank -7.763    0/20   p = 0.0000

  lateral vs off     linear   +0.028   13/20   p = 0.0297
                     corr     -0.004    3/20   p = 0.0033
                     eff_rank -0.265    7/20   p = 0.0899

  lateral+on vs on   linear   +0.037   15/20   p = 0.0005
                     corr     +0.001   12/20   p = 0.4005
                     eff_rank -0.258    4/20   p = 0.0030

PREDICTION ONE: CORRECT, ALL THREE NUMBERS
-------------------------------------------
`on vs off` was called at corr +0.05 to +0.09, eff_rank down 5 to 8, linear
~+0.07. Measured: +0.077, -7.763, +0.074. The one-seed observation was not
noise, and the effect is as structural as it looked.

**Salience-gated Hebbian binding raises correlation by 43% and cuts effective
rank almost in half -- from 17.4 of 96 to 9.6 -- while raising linear
decodability by +0.074.** That is now a 20-seed result at p = 0.0000 on all
three metrics, and 20/20 and 0/20 seeds respectively on the two shape metrics:
there is no seed where it goes the other way.

So the premise that put lateral inhibition at the top of the list is refuted.
The representation is not redundant and decorrelating is not what helps. The
mechanism that works **consolidates** -- it collapses the state onto fewer,
more strongly co-varying directions, and that is what makes it linearly
readable.

PREDICTION THREE: REFUTED, AND IT IS THE ONE THAT DECIDED THE DIRECTION
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
`lateral+on vs on` was called NEGATIVE or null, on the reasoning that a rule
fighting consolidation should not help. It is **+0.037 at p = 0.0005, better on
15 of 20 seeds.**

That is the branch the prediction named as what would save the mechanism, so
the consequence written down in advance does not fire: lateral inhibition stays.

Prediction two was also wrong, in the same direction. `lateral vs off` was
called null at |diff| < 0.02, p > 0.05. It is +0.028 at p = 0.0297.

The gains are close to additive:

  off          0.802
  + lateral    0.830   (+0.028)
  + binding    0.876   (+0.074)
  + both       0.913   (+0.111, against +0.102 if they simply summed)

Two mechanisms, two roughly independent channels, and together they take linear
decodability past 0.9 for the first time in this project.

BUT THE ROUTE IS REFUTED, AND THAT MATTERS MORE THAN THE WIN
-------------------------------------------------------------
The prediction's escape hatch was "consolidate the signal, decorrelate the
residue" -- complementary because lateral cleans up what binding leaves. The
correlation and effective-rank columns say that is not what happened:

  lateral alone     corr 0.178 -> 0.175, a 2.2% relative move
                    eff_rank 17.40 -> 17.14, a 1.5% move, and null at p = 0.09
                    linear +0.028, significant

  lateral on top    corr unchanged (p = 0.40)
  of binding        eff_rank 9.641 -> 9.383, i.e. DOWN -- the opposite of
                    decorrelation, and significant at p = 0.0030
                    linear +0.037, significant

**Lateral inhibition improves decodability while moving neither decorrelation
metric in the direction decorrelation predicts.** Added to binding it makes the
representation slightly *lower*-rank, not higher. Whatever this rule is doing,
the two quantities that were supposed to explain it do not.

So the honest state of it is: the mechanism is kept on its measurement, and the
reasoning that put it on the list is recorded as refuted. Those are separate
findings and collapsing them would be the exact failure this sweep was written
to avoid -- a premise surviving its own disconfirming measurement because the
outcome happened to come out well.

What lateral is actually doing is now unmeasured and open. It is anti-Hebbian
on inhibitory synapses, so the reachable hypotheses include: sharpening which
units respond at all rather than how they covary (`sparsity` is flat at 0.031
and 0.034, so not a gross activity change); increasing the dynamic range of the
surviving units; or acting as a second consolidating force by a different
route, which the eff_rank direction mildly favours. None of these is measured
and none should be written down as the explanation.

WHAT THIS COSTS THE CHAIN THAT LED HERE
----------------------------------------
Sweep 011 found RLS gains by carrying a pooled inverse correlation matrix, and
that the gain does not decompose per column. That measurement stands. The
*inference* drawn from it -- "therefore the column needs decorrelating" -- does
not follow and is now measured false. RLS pooling correlations is a fact about
RLS, not a diagnosis of the representation.

Sweep 019's contribution stands entirely: the readout is the slow part. What
this sweep removes is the specific mechanism proposed to fix it, along with the
reason for proposing it.

WHAT IS STILL UNTESTED, AND IS THE ACTUAL POINT
------------------------------------------------
Lateral inhibition was built to let a *cheap local readout* extract what RLS
extracts by pooling. This sweep measured only the offline representation. It did
not run a single end-to-end condition with lateral enabled, so the payoff
question is untouched.

And sweep 016 is the standing warning against assuming: binding's +0.074
offline gain arrived end to end as +0.007. An offline gain of +0.037 is not
evidence of anything the online readout can use.

That is sweep 021, and it has a clean form: lateral+binding under the shipped
delta readout, against binding under RLS. If the delta readout with local
lateral inhibition reaches what RLS reaches, the destination was right even
though the route was wrong, and the locality exception RLS forces can be
dropped. If it does not, lateral is a +0.037 offline gain with no demonstrated
end-to-end value, and should be described as exactly that.
