Sweep 021 -- does lateral inhibition pay anything the model can actually use?
=============================================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Our best-performing readout cheats a little: it needs to look at all the neurons
at once, which is exactly the thing this architecture is supposed to avoid,
because it doesn't work when the network is spread across machines.

Lateral inhibition was added to make that cheat unnecessary -- the idea being
that if neurons tidy up their own signals locally, a simple readout could manage
on its own.

This measures whether that worked: does the simple readout with the new mechanism
reach what the cheating readout reaches? If not, the cheat stays, and the idea
gets closed rather than tinkered with.

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 020 measured lateral inhibition offline and it helped: +0.028 alone and
+0.037 on top of binding, taking linear decodability to 0.913. It also refuted
the reason it was built. It does not decorrelate -- correlation moves by -0.004
alone and not at all on top of binding, and effective rank goes *down* rather
than up.

That leaves the mechanism standing on a measurement with no explanation, and
leaves the thing it was built for completely untested. Lateral inhibition was
proposed so that a cheap local readout could extract what RLS extracts by
pooling a correlation matrix that sweep 011 showed does not decompose. Sweep 020
ran no end-to-end condition at all.

Sweep 016 is the standing reason not to assume: binding's +0.074 offline gain
arrived end to end as +0.007. Offline decodability and accuracy have come apart
in both directions in this project already.

CONDITIONS  (all 300 episodes, 20 seeds, 96 neurons, column lr 0)
------------------------------------------------------------------
Existing, as the paired baselines:

  e2e-off      0.709   nothing on, delta readout
  e2e-on       0.715   binding, delta readout
  rls-off      0.759   nothing on, RLS readout
  rls-on       0.803   binding, RLS readout
  catchup-off  0.707   nothing on, +150 readout-only episodes
  catchup-on   0.770   binding, +150 readout-only episodes

New:

  lat-on           lateral only, delta readout
  latbind-on       lateral + binding, delta readout
  latbind-rls      lateral + binding, RLS readout
  latbind-catchup  lateral + binding, +150 readout-only episodes

**`latbind-on` against `rls-on` is the whole sweep.** If a local rule under the
shipped delta readout reaches what a pooled correlation matrix reaches, the
destination was right even though the route was wrong, and the one standing
exception to the locality rule can be dropped.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
------------------------------------
The offline-to-online transfer ratio is measurable from what binding already
did. Binding's offline gain over off is +0.074, and it arrived as:

  delta readout    +0.007   ratio 0.09
  RLS readout      +0.043   ratio 0.58
  catch-up         +0.063   ratio 0.85

Lateral + binding is +0.111 offline over off. Applying the same ratios:

  lat-on           0.705 to 0.725, NULL against e2e-off (p > 0.05)
  latbind-on       0.715 to 0.735, NULL against e2e-on
  latbind-rls      0.810 to 0.835, beating rls-off, probably not rls-on
  latbind-catchup  0.790 to 0.820, beating catchup-on

So: **`latbind-on` null, and nowhere near `rls-on`'s 0.803.** Confidence high,
and the reason is mechanical rather than extrapolated. The argument for
decorrelation helping a delta rule is that gradient descent converges slowly on
correlated features, which is exactly what RLS fixes by whitening. Sweep 020
measured lateral not decorrelating -- so the property that would have made the
delta readout converge faster is absent, and there is no mechanism left by which
this should work. Sweep 019 adds the second reason: the delta readout's problem
is sample efficiency against a *settled* column, and lateral is one more rule
keeping the column moving.

FAILURE RESPONSE, NAMED IN ADVANCE
-----------------------------------
**If `latbind-on` is null against `e2e-on`, the "local decorrelation replaces
RLS" programme is closed and recorded as closed.** Not parked pending a rate
sweep. The premise was refuted in 020 and the payoff would then have failed in
021, which is two independent measurements against it. The README stops naming
it as the next step and the TODO item is replaced by what is actually open: what
lateral is doing, and why homeostatic settling alone buys +0.197.

Lateral inhibition itself stays either way -- it is a measured +0.037 on the
representation. What closes is the claim about what it is for.

WHAT WOULD SAVE IT
-------------------
`latbind-on` at 0.77 or above, i.e. reaching catch-up territory with no
catch-up phase. That would mean lateral helps the readout track a representation
that is still moving, which is a different and more interesting property than
decorrelation, and would need measuring directly rather than inferring from
accuracy.

WHAT THIS SWEEP DROPS, AND WHY
-------------------------------
The trajectory probe (019) and the three schedule conditions (018: decay-99,
decay-98, phase) come out of the matrix. Both questions are answered and
recorded, and re-running them costs roughly six minutes of every seed to
reproduce numbers already written down. The sweep notes are the record; CI is
for open questions.

A CHANGE THIS SWEEP FORCED
---------------------------
The catch-up condition froze the column by setting `cfg.hebbian = False`. That
was correct while binding was the only rule writing to W. Adding a second one
would have made every `latbind-catchup` run report "the readout converged
against a static column" while lateral inhibition kept changing it underneath --
a condition whose name was false, which is the failure mode this project is
built against rather than an inconvenience.

Fixed structurally rather than by remembering: `freeze_plasticity` clears every
registered flag, and `test_freeze_plasticity_accounts_for_every_mechanism_flag`
enumerates ColumnConfig's boolean fields and fails if one is in neither
`PLASTICITY_FLAGS` nor `NON_PLASTICITY_FLAGS`. A future mechanism cannot be
added without deciding what "frozen" means for it. Both are in
`experiments/mutation.py`.

No recorded result is affected: every catch-up condition ever run had lateral
off and `lr=0`, so there was nothing for the omission to leak.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, column lr 0, drain-steps 0, lateral_lr at
its default 0.02 -- the value sweep 020 measured, not retuned for this.

THE CENTRAL PREDICTION HELD, AND THE DIRECTION CLOSES
------------------------------------------------------
`latbind-on` was predicted null against `e2e-on` and nowhere near `rls-on`. It
is +0.016 at p = 0.33, and **reliably worse than `rls-on` -- -0.071, better on
only 5 of 20 seeds, p = 0.0012.**

`lat-on` was predicted 0.705-0.725 and null. It is 0.707 at p = 0.90, a paired
difference of -0.001.

So the consequence named in advance fires. **The "local decorrelation replaces
RLS" programme is closed.** Sweep 020 refuted the premise -- lateral inhibition
does not decorrelate -- and 021 refutes the payoff: under the shipped delta
readout it does not approach what a pooled correlation matrix reaches. Two
independent measurements against it, and the honest response is to stop rather
than to sweep `lateral_lr` until something crosses significance.

The locality exception RLS forces stays. That remains the one place in this
model where an operation needs the whole population, and nothing local has been
shown to replace it.

BUT LATERAL INHIBITION PAYS -- THROUGH THE READOUT IT WAS BUILT TO REPLACE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
`latbind-rls` reaches **0.842, beating `rls-on` at 0.803 by +0.040 on 15 of 20
seeds, p = 0.0284.** That is the best end-to-end number in this project, and
+0.083 over `rls-off`.

Predicted at 0.810-0.835 and "probably not beating rls-on". Wrong on both, in
the same direction.

The irony is exact and worth stating plainly: a mechanism built to make RLS
unnecessary turns out to work only when RLS is present.

AND THE CATCH-UP PREDICTION WAS WRONG, WHICH IS THE INFORMATIVE PART
---------------------------------------------------------------------
`latbind-catchup` was predicted 0.790-0.820, beating `catchup-on`. It is 0.775
against 0.770 -- a paired difference of +0.005 at p = 0.60. Nothing.

Line the transfer ratios up. Offline gain, then what fraction of it survives
end to end under each readout:

  binding (+0.074 offline)      catchup 0.85   RLS 0.58   delta 0.09
  lateral on top (+0.037)       catchup 0.14   RLS 1.08   delta 0.43 (null)

**The ordering inverts.** Binding's gain transfers best when the readout is
given a settled column and worst under the delta rule. Lateral's transfers
essentially completely under RLS and not at all under catch-up.

That is a real structural difference between the two mechanisms and it was not
predicted by anything. It says the readout's difficulty with lateral's
contribution is not *time* -- catch-up gives it 150 episodes against a static
column and it collects nothing.

A HYPOTHESIS, LABELLED AS ONE
------------------------------
What distinguishes RLS from a delta rule given unlimited time is whitening: RLS
carries an inverse correlation matrix, so it can read directions that are
correlated with existing ones. A delta rule cannot, however long it runs,
because gradient descent on correlated features converges along the dominant
directions first and the rest arbitrarily slowly.

Sweep 020 measured lateral inhibition *not* reducing correlation. If what it
adds therefore sits in directions correlated with those already present, then
exactly this pattern follows: recoverable by whitening, invisible to a delta
rule no matter how settled the column is.

That is consistent with both sweeps and it is **not measured**. It is written
here as a hypothesis with a test, not as a finding. The test: project the
column state onto its top-k principal directions and measure how much of the
lateral condition's decodability advantage survives. If the hypothesis holds,
lateral's gain should live disproportionately in low-variance directions and
binding's in high-variance ones.

WHAT GOES IN THE README
------------------------
  established   lateral inhibition improves the representation (+0.037 offline,
                sweep 020) and pays end to end under an RLS readout (+0.040,
                p = 0.0284), reaching 0.842 -- the best number in the project
  established   it does NOT pay under the shipped delta readout, with or
                without a catch-up phase, and does not approach RLS there
  refuted       that local decorrelation could replace RLS's pooled matrix
  refuted       that lateral inhibition decorrelates at all (sweep 020)
  open          why its gain is readable by whitening and not by time

The default configuration ships the delta readout, so **the 0.842 is not what
the model does today.** Quote it as what it does with RLS, and RLS remains a
documented exception to the locality rule.

NOT DOING THE OBVIOUS THING
----------------------------
The obvious move is to sweep `lateral_lr` looking for a rate where the delta
readout does collect the gain. The prediction ruled that out in advance and it
stands: a premise that survives its own disconfirming measurement by acquiring
hyperparameters is how a project spends a month on nothing. The rate was not
retuned for this sweep and will not be retuned to rescue it.
