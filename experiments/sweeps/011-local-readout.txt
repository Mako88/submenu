Sweep 011 (local measurement, 3 seeds) -- can the RLS gain be had locally?

RLS gave the readout +0.032 at p = 0.0098, the project's first significant
positive, but it buys that with an (n+1)x(n+1) inverse correlation matrix pooled
across every neuron. In a split model that state would have to be assembled from
every machine, which is exactly what the design rule forbids.

Two local variants were built to find out which half of RLS does the work --
the per-dimension adaptive step size, or the decorrelation between dimensions:

    delta          0.598    fully local
    rls_diag       0.500    fully local, one scalar per input
    rls_block=6    0.584    local to a machine, blocks of 6 neurons
    rls_block=12   0.598    local to a machine, blocks of 12
    rls (full)     0.658    pooled across all 24 neurons

The answer is decorrelation, and it does not decompose. Per-dimension adaptive
rates alone are worse than a plain delta rule -- rls_diag lands at chance.
Decorrelating within blocks of half the population recovers nothing at all.
Only the full matrix helps.

So the honest conclusion is that this gain cannot be bought locally at the
readout, and the default goes back to delta. Trading the architecture's central
property for +0.032 on a toy task is a bad deal.

The finding does point somewhere better, though. If the column's outputs were
already decorrelated, a local readout would suffice -- and decorrelation by
lateral inhibition is both local and exactly what inhibitory interneurons do in
cortex and olfactory bulb. That moves the operation from the readout, where it
cannot be local, into the network, where it can.
