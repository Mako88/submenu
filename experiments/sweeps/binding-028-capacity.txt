Sweep 028 -- does the binding gain survive a bigger column?
===========================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Every number in this project comes from a column of 96 neurons. If the one
mechanism that works only works at that size, it is a curiosity rather than a
result, and everything built on top of it has to be re-examined.

So: run the same comparison at 48, 96, 192 and 384 neurons and see whether the
gain grows, holds, or vanishes.

There is a trap, and it is the reason this sweep has a second half. The way the
gain is measured is by fitting a simple decoder to the network's state. A bigger
network means more numbers to fit, and with a fixed amount of data a decoder
given more numbers starts memorising rather than generalising. That would make
the gain look like it grows for a reason that has nothing to do with the
network. The second half holds the data-per-neuron ratio constant so the two
explanations can be told apart.

THE CONFOUND, WHICH THE PILOT MADE VISIBLE
--------------------------------------------
A one-seed pilot at 120 episodes and 250 collection episodes:

  neurons     off      on     gain
       48   0.800   0.827   +0.027
       96   0.800   0.840   +0.040
      192   0.747   0.947   +0.200

The gain appears to grow sharply. It cannot be reported that way, for two
reasons visible in the same table.

**The decoder is unregularised and runs out of samples.** `probe.logistic` uses
`l2 = 1e-4`, which is nearly none. At 250 collection episodes a 70/30 split
leaves 175 training samples. Against 192 features that is p > n, so `off-192`
falling to 0.747 while smaller columns hold 0.800 is what decoder overfitting
looks like, not what a worse representation looks like.

**And binding changes exactly the quantity that determines overfitting.** Sweep
020 measured binding *halving* effective rank -- 17.4 to 9.6 at 96 neurons. A
representation living in fewer effective dimensions is intrinsically less prone
to overfitting a small sample. So at large N the measured "gain" conflates

  binding makes the representation better
  binding makes the representation lower-dimensional, so the decoder copes

and the second grows with column size *by construction*. This would produce a
rising gain with no improvement in the representation at all.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 300 episodes, column lr 0)
---------------------------------------------------
  off-48   on-48     |
  off-96   on-96     |  collect 1200 for every size
  off-192  on-192    |  samples/feature: 17.5, 8.8, 4.4, 2.2
  off-384  on-384    |

  off-48-thin  on-48-thin    collect 150 -- samples/feature 2.2, matching 384

`*-thin` is the control and the sweep turns on it. It runs the **smallest**
column at the **same samples-per-feature as the largest**. If the rising gain is
decoder overfitting, `48-thin` should show a large gain too, because it has the
same starved decoder. If the gain at 48 stays small when starved, overfitting is
not what produces the gain at 384, and the growth is real.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
--------------------------------------
Made on mechanism, not on the pilot. A single seed has produced three claims
that twenty seeds corrected in this project (020's ordering, 023's sign, 024's
magnitude), so the pilot is treated as evidence the plumbing works and nothing
more.

  gain at 48     +0.03 to +0.07
  gain at 96     +0.074   (established, sweeps 015-025)
  gain at 192    +0.06 to +0.11
  gain at 384    +0.05 to +0.12
  gain at 48-thin  +0.03 to +0.08 -- i.e. **the same as 48**, not inflated

**The central claim: the gain holds roughly constant across a factor of 8 in
column size, and `48-thin` matches `48`.** The +0.200 in the pilot is expected
to shrink to something near the standing +0.074.

The reasoning: binding strengthens the excitatory synapses that were driving a
neuron, and fan-in is fixed at 8 branches x 16 synapses regardless of column
size. Each neuron's local computation is therefore *identical* at every size --
only the number of neurons and the pool of sources change. A mechanism whose
per-neuron operation does not change with N should not have an N-dependent
effect, so a flat gain is what the architecture predicts.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS
-------------------------
  flat gain          The mechanism is size-independent, which is what it should
                     be, and the 96-neuron results generalise. Boring and
                     important.

  gain vanishes      The mechanism is a small-column artefact. Sweeps 015-027
                     describe a regime nothing would ever be deployed in, and
                     the binding results need re-stating as size-specific.

  gain grows, and    Something about a larger source pool makes binding more
  48-thin is small   effective. Genuinely interesting and unexplained, and the
                     next question is whether it keeps growing or saturates.

  gain grows, and    Decoder overfitting. Not a result about the model at all,
  48-thin is large   and every cross-size comparison in this sweep is void --
                     including, uncomfortably, the possibility that some of the
                     standing +0.074 at 96 neurons is the same effect at 8.8
                     samples per feature. That would need its own sweep.

The last one is the reason the control exists, and it is the outcome that would
cost the most to discover later.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, column lr 0, binding on/off at 48/96/192/384 neurons
with 1200 collection episodes, plus 48 at 150 collection episodes. Fan-in fixed
at the default 8 branches x 16 synapses everywhere, so per-neuron computation is
identical across sizes and only N varies.

THE CONTROL CAME BACK CLEAN, WHICH IS THE MAIN RESULT
-------------------------------------------------------
`48-thin` was the condition this sweep turns on: the smallest column at the
largest column's samples-per-feature, so that decoder starvation could be told
apart from a real effect. Its gain is **+0.052 at p = 0.0684 -- smaller than
`48`'s +0.078 and not significant.** Starving the decoder did not inflate the
gain, so the outcome named as "would cost the most to discover later" did not
occur, and the cross-size comparisons stand.

**The pilot's rising gain was decoder starvation.** At 250 collection episodes
one seed showed +0.027 / +0.040 / +0.200 across 48 / 96 / 192. At 1200 it is
+0.078 / +0.060 / +0.066 -- flat. The prediction called this, and called it from
the mechanism: fan-in is fixed at 8 x 16 regardless of column size, so each
neuron's local computation is identical at every N and a flat gain is what the
architecture requires. That is the second consecutive sweep (with 027) where a
mechanistic prediction written in advance beat the pilot it was checking.

WHERE THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG, AND WHY IT MATTERS MORE THAN WHERE IT WAS RIGHT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  predicted  +0.03..+0.07 at 48     measured +0.078   just above the band
  predicted  +0.074       at 96     measured +0.060   below
  predicted  +0.06..+0.11 at 192    measured +0.066   in band
  predicted  +0.05..+0.12 at 384    measured +0.020   **well below the band**
  predicted  same as 48   at thin   measured +0.052   in band, but null

The miss at 384 is not a miss about binding. `off-384` reaches **0.973**, and
`on-384` 0.993. There is 0.027 of headroom in the metric, so a gain of +0.05
was not available to be measured. The prediction quoted a band without checking
whether the ceiling admitted it -- the same class of error as asserting a bound
an operating range never approaches (standards rule 9), committed in a
prediction instead of a test.

**So this sweep answers the size question up to 192 and cannot answer it above
that, because the task runs out before the column does.** The saturation is
visible in three places at once:

  off eff_rank    15.8 -> 17.6 -> 19.9 -> 20.5    8x the neurons, +29% rank
  on  eff_rank     9.3 ->  9.6 -> 10.3 -> 10.4    8x the neurons, +11% rank
  mlp             0.976 -> 0.993 -> 0.997 -> 0.999

An 8-fold increase in column size buys 4.7 extra effective dimensions with
binding off and 1.1 with it on. Neither is running out of *column*; both are
running out of things for a delayed-XOR episode to be about. Any future capacity
question needs three-cue parity or another task with more to say, and re-running
this matrix on delayed XOR at 1024 would produce two numbers near 1.0 and no
information.

THE OTHER THING THE `on` COLUMN SAYS
--------------------------------------
`on` eff_rank is 9.3, 9.6, 10.3, 10.4 across a factor of eight. **Binding pins
the representation to about ten effective dimensions almost regardless of column
size.** Sweep 020 measured the halving at 96 neurons and read it as
consolidation; this shows it is not a halving at all but a *fixed ceiling* that
merely happens to be half of 17.6 at N = 96.

That is a stronger and less comfortable statement than 020's. It is good where
the readout is starved and the task is small -- which is every measurement in
this project -- and it is exactly what would stop the mechanism scaling to a task
needing more than ten dimensions. Whether the ceiling is a property of binding or
of this task is not determined by this sweep and is the first question item 1
should ask next.

A BASELINE MOVED AGAIN, AND THIS TIME IT WAS ON PURPOSE
---------------------------------------------------------
`off-96` reads **0.853** here against the 0.802 that sweeps 015-026 measure, and
`on-96` 0.913 against 0.876. The cause is known and intended: `collect` is 1200
rather than 500, so the decoder has 2.4x the training data. Sweep 026 lost a run
to an unintended version of exactly this.

The consequence is that **the standing +0.074 at 96 neurons is a
sample-size-dependent number, not a property of the column.** At 500 collection
episodes it is +0.074; at 1200 it is +0.060. Neither is wrong, and the gap is
the part of the gain that comes from binding making the representation cheaper
to fit rather than better. Every "+0.074" in the repo now needs its collection
budget attached to it, and does not transfer to a differently-sized probe.
