Sweep 024 -- does learning a second task cost what the column learned on the first?
===================================================================================

QUESTION
--------
Every number in this repo is a number about one task with one answer and no
interference between memories. That is TODO item 1 and the largest gap in the
project: delayed XOR cannot ask about capacity and cannot ask about forgetting.

It also makes sweep 014's refutation of engram allocation narrower than it
reads. The allocator's whole purpose was to push successive memories onto
*different* neurons, following Han et al. 2007 / Yiu et al. 2014 / Cai et al.
2016, and it demonstrably produced engrams overlapping less than independent
sampling (`engram-012`, rotate 0.815 against 1.661). A benchmark with one memory
never asks for that, so 014 refuted allocation *on delayed XOR* and not in
general. This is the smallest benchmark that asks.

THE BENCHMARK
--------------
Two tasks that are the same computation over a different sensory mapping:
delayed XOR with the input channels permuted. Identical difficulty, identical
statistics, disjoint in what they ask the column's fixed wiring to do.

  train on A  ->  measure A            acquisition
  train on B  ->  measure A and B      retention, and whether B learned at all

The permutation is what makes this a benchmark rather than two tasks. Per
timestep the total input energy is *identical* between A and B, and the multiset
of per-channel totals is identical, so the two cannot differ in any quantity
that ignores which channel is which. If B were simply harder, forgetting and
difficulty would not be separable in the result. Asserted by
`test_channel_permutation_is_a_relabelling_and_not_a_harder_task`, whose third
assertion is that the mappings genuinely differ -- without it the two variants
would be one task and retention would be trivially perfect.

Decodability is measured by an offline decoder fit fresh each time, so the
online readout is not involved and this is a statement about the column's
representation.

A CLAIM THIS SWEEP ALREADY FALSIFIED, BEFORE IT RAN
-----------------------------------------------------
The experiment's docstring asserted that `off` cannot forget, "because nothing
about a frozen column changes". The first run refuted it: `off` lost **0.167**
at one seed.

A column with `lr=0` and no binding still runs threshold homeostasis, knee
adaptation and synaptic scaling. A and B have identical marginal statistics, but
each neuron sees a different subset of channels through its fixed wiring, so its
own drive distribution changes when the mapping changes and its threshold
follows. Sweep 022 measured that operating point carrying most of the column's
representation quality -- so it is precisely the thing that then moves.

**"Frozen" has been used interchangeably with "unchanging" throughout this
project and it is neither.** Sweep 019 already caught one version of this
(frozen is not untrained). This is the second, and the docstring is corrected.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 150 episodes per task, 96 neurons, column lr 0)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
  off            homeostasis and scaling only
  on             salience-gated Hebbian binding
  lateral        lateral inhibition only
  both           binding + lateral

PREDICTION (recorded before the 20-seed run)
----------------------------------------------
One seed is in hand and the prediction is informed by it, as with sweeps 020,
022 and 023.

  off        retention -0.10 to -0.20, acquisition +0.08 to +0.14
  on         retention -0.04 to +0.04, acquisition +0.15 to +0.25
  lateral    retention between the two, closer to off
  both       retention like `on`, acquisition highest

**The central claim: binding retains better than `off`, and the difference is
significant.** One seed puts it at +0.017 against -0.167. Predicting the effect
survives at 20 seeds but shrinks -- call it a paired difference of +0.08 to
+0.15, p < 0.05.

The reasoning, so it can be wrong for a stated reason: binding writes into `W`,
which is a much larger store than the per-neuron threshold vector and is
modified only on modulator release rather than continuously. If A's structure is
held in `W` and the forgetting mechanism is `theta` drifting, then a column with
binding has somewhere to keep A that the drift does not reach.

WHAT WOULD SURPRISE ME, AND WHAT IT WOULD MEAN
------------------------------------------------
`on` retaining *worse* than `off`. Binding is unsupervised and fires on every
modulator release, so it writes task B's structure into the same `W` that holds
A's -- classic catastrophic interference, and the standard expectation for an
undifferentiated Hebbian store. If that is what happens, **this is the benchmark
that gives engram allocation its rematch**, because separating which neurons
carry which memory is the textbook fix and 014 refuted it on a task that could
not show the difference.

I am predicting against that outcome and would find it the more interesting
result.

A CONFOUND, NAMED
------------------
`retention` is a within-condition difference (`a_after - a_before`), so a
condition that acquired A less well has less to lose. At one seed `off` starts
at 0.900 and `on` at 0.833, which flatters `on`'s retention through regression
to the mean. The paired test is per seed and per condition, which controls it
across seeds but not within one -- so `a_before` is reported alongside, and if
the two conditions differ there by more than ~0.03 at 20 seeds, retention needs
reading as a pair with acquisition rather than alone.

WHAT THIS SWEEP CANNOT SAY
---------------------------
Nothing about capacity -- two tasks is not a stream. Nothing about whether the
forgetting mechanism is really threshold drift; that is a direct measurement
(correlate per-neuron |dtheta| against per-neuron contribution to A's decoder)
and it is the obvious follow-up if `off` forgets as much as one seed suggests.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 150 episodes per task, 400 probe episodes, 96 neurons, column lr 0.
Channel seeds fixed at 1 and 2 for every model seed, so every seed faces the
same pair of tasks and a hard permutation does not become invisible seed
variance.

A CLAIM I PROPAGATED FROM ONE SEED, AND IT IS WRONG
-----------------------------------------------------
This sweep's own write-up said, in bold, that the first run "refuted a claim I
had written into the experiment's own docstring" -- that a frozen column cannot
forget -- because `off` lost **0.167** at seed 0. That number went into the
experiment docstring, TODO.md, AUDIT.md and a commit message.

At 20 seeds `off` loses **0.015**. An order of magnitude smaller.

Two separate things were bundled together and only one survives:

  the mechanism   A frozen column *does* change when its input changes.
                  Homeostasis, knee adaptation and synaptic scaling keep
                  running, each neuron's drive distribution shifts when the
                  channel mapping does, and its threshold follows. Still true,
                  and now pinned by
                  `test_a_frozen_column_still_changes_when_the_input_changes`.

  the magnitude   "A frozen column forgets 0.167." **False.** One seed.

Corrected everywhere it was written. This is the third time in this project a
single seed has produced a confident claim that twenty seeds reversed or
shrank -- sweep 020's ordering, sweep 023's sign, and now this one's size. The
first two were predictions, which is survivable. This one got into four files as
a statement of fact, which is the failure rule 1 exists to prevent.

**Nothing is measured until it is measured at twenty seeds, including the
observations made while building the instrument.**

EVERY RETENTION COMPARISON IS NULL, AND THE PREDICTION IS REFUTED
-------------------------------------------------------------------
The central claim was that binding retains better than `off` by +0.08 to +0.15
at p < 0.05. It retains -0.012 *worse*, at p = 0.35. Lateral: -0.015, p = 0.32.
Both: -0.008, p = 0.64. Binding against both: +0.004, p = 0.81.

**No condition forgets differently from any other.** Forgetting is small and
uniform: every condition lands between -0.015 and -0.030.

THE ENGRAM REMATCH DOES NOT TRIGGER, FOR THE OPPOSITE REASON TO THE ONE NAMED
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The prediction named binding-retains-worse-than-off as the outcome that would
give engram allocation its rematch, on the reasoning that an undifferentiated
Hebbian store should show catastrophic interference and separating which neurons
carry which memory is the textbook fix.

That is not what happened. Binding does not interfere catastrophically -- it
does not interfere detectably at all. **There is no forgetting here for
allocation to fix.** Two tasks, 150 episodes each, 96 neurons: the benchmark
finds no interference to study.

So allocation stays deleted, and the reason it stays deleted is now better than
it was. Sweep 014 refuted it on a task that could not ask; this one asked and
found the question does not bite at this scale.

WHAT THE BENCHMARK DID FIND, AND IT IS NOT NOTHING
----------------------------------------------------
The instrument works -- it resolves large differences at p = 0.0001. They are
all in **acquisition**, not retention:

  off 0.269  ->  lateral 0.310  ->  on 0.325  ->  both 0.353

and in `a_before`, where binding beats off on **20 of 20 seeds** at p = 0.0000.
That is an independent replication of the standing binding and lateral results
on a task variant no previous sweep used, which is worth having: every prior
number for those mechanisms came from one channel mapping.

WHY THERE IS NO FORGETTING, STATED AS A HYPOTHESIS
----------------------------------------------------
Sweeps 022-023 found the column's representation quality is carried by an
operating point -- per-neuron threshold and knee -- and that the *best* such
point is found on input with no task structure at all. If what the column holds
is largely task-independent, then switching tasks has little to disturb, and
that is exactly what this sweep measures.

That reading is consistent with both sweeps and is **not tested here**. The
direct test: correlate per-neuron |dtheta| across the task switch against each
neuron's weight in task A's decoder. If forgetting is threshold drift, the
neurons that moved most should be the ones A depended on.

WHAT WOULD MAKE THIS BENCHMARK BITE
-------------------------------------
Not a fix -- a harder setting, and the sweep should be re-run rather than the
conclusion stretched. Candidates, in order of how much they would sharpen it:
a longer stream than two tasks; a smaller column, since 96 neurons for one
delayed-XOR variant is not obviously capacity-limited; and more episodes per
task so there is more to overwrite. Sweep 014's caveat still stands and now has
a number attached: allocation is refuted on benchmarks that do not create
interference, and nothing here has created any.
