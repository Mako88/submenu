Sweep 016 -- does the representation gain survive the readout?
==============================================================

QUESTION
--------
Everything measured about salience-gated Hebbian binding so far is
representation quality: an offline logistic decoder on the column state reads
0.876 with it against 0.802 without (sweep 014, confirmed against the rewritten
code in 015). Nothing has scored the model end to end, by the online readout it
actually ships with.

Sweep 009 is why that is not a formality. It measured a column-level quantity
that the online readout could not see, and the whole point of that sweep was
that representation and accuracy come apart. It happened to come apart in the
direction that hid a *loss* then; there is no reason it cannot hide a gain now.

CONDITIONS
----------
  e2e-off   frozen column, hebbian off
  e2e-on    frozen column, hebbian on

Column lr 0 in both, so the three-factor rule contributes nothing and this
isolates binding. Same 20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons as sweeps 014/015.
drain_steps pinned to 0 rather than e2e.py's default of 200: that default
exists to make *lag* comparisons valid, and with no lag it only adds 200 silent
steps per episode, shifting the operating point away from the sweeps this is
meant to be comparable with. Sweep 013 is the reason that is worth stating.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
------------------------------------
Moderate confidence: **+0.02 to +0.05 on eval accuracy, p < 0.05.**

The reasoning, so the number is checkable rather than a feeling. The online
delta readout has always extracted less than an offline decoder on the same
states -- the README records 0.717 end to end at 96 neurons against ~0.85
offline, so it captures roughly 60% of the available margin above chance. If
that fraction holds, a +0.074 offline gain should arrive as roughly +0.045.
Predicting a range below that because assuming linear transfer is the
optimistic case, and because a readout tracking a representation that is itself
drifting under binding is a harder problem than either alone.

`e2e-off` should land near 0.71 -- the README's figure at this size, though at
350 episodes rather than 300.

FAILURE RESPONSES, NAMED IN ADVANCE
------------------------------------
A null here is the likely outcome to mishandle, so:

  If p > 0.05 with a positive point estimate, the readout is the suspect, not
  the mechanism. The specific test is to re-run with --readout-rule rls, which
  sweep 010 showed closes the online/offline extraction gap (+0.032, p =
  0.0098). If the gain appears under RLS and not under delta, binding works and
  the delta readout cannot see it -- which is a statement about the readout and
  would make local decorrelation inside the column the next thing to build.
  That is a real experiment, not a way of explaining the null away, and it
  should be run before the mechanism is credited end to end.

  If the difference is significantly NEGATIVE, that is the interesting case and
  it should not be softened. It would mean binding helps a decoder fitted after
  the fact while hurting one learning online, most plausibly because binding
  moves the representation under a readout that is chasing it. Testable by
  freezing the column's binding partway through training and letting the
  readout converge against a static representation.

  What must not happen either way: reporting the offline number as though it
  established end-to-end performance. It does not, and until this sweep lands
  the README should not say otherwise.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds
-------------------------
condition   eval accuracy
e2e-off     0.709 +/- 0.054
e2e-on      0.715 +/- 0.069

  +0.007, 11/20 seeds, permutation p = 0.6834.  NULL.

The prediction was wrong. It said +0.02 to +0.05 at p < 0.05, reasoning that
the online readout captures ~60% of the offline margin and should therefore
deliver ~60% of a +0.074 gain. It delivered 9%.

`e2e-off` at 0.709 was predicted at ~0.71, so the baseline is exactly where it
should be. Nothing is wrong with the harness; the gain simply does not arrive.

WHAT THIS COSTS
---------------
Sweep 009's warning was correct and now runs in the other direction. The
+0.074 is a fact about an offline decoder fitted to the final column state. It
is not, on this evidence, a fact about the model's accuracy. Every claim about
this mechanism has to carry that distinction until something closes the gap.

The README said "first positive learning result" on the strength of the
offline number. That was written before this sweep and is now too strong; it
has been corrected rather than softened.

One detail worth noticing rather than smoothing over: the `on` condition has
*higher* seed variance than `off` (0.069 against 0.054) despite a nearly
identical mean. Binding is doing something -- it is not inert -- and what it
does helps on some seeds and hurts on others. That is the signature of a
readout struggling with a representation that moved during training, not of a
mechanism with no effect.

FOLLOW-UP, AS NAMED IN ADVANCE
-------------------------------
The recorded response to "null with a positive point estimate" was: the
readout is the suspect, and the test is RLS. That is sweep 017, and a second
condition has been added alongside it because the two candidate explanations
are distinguishable and it would be careless to run only one:

  1. EXTRACTION. The delta readout cannot pull out what is there. Sweep 010
     showed RLS closes the online/offline gap (+0.032, p = 0.0098). If the
     binding gain appears under RLS and not delta, the mechanism works and the
     shipped readout cannot see it.

  2. NON-STATIONARITY. The offline decoder is fitted to the *final* column
     state. The online readout learns while binding is still changing that
     state underneath it, so it spends its budget chasing a moving target.
     Tested by freezing binding after training and letting the readout
     converge against a now-static column: if accuracy then rises toward the
     offline number, the mechanism is fine and the training schedule is what
     is wrong.

These are not alternatives to accepting the null -- the null stands either
way. They ask *why* it is null, and the two answers imply completely different
next steps: local decorrelation inside the column for (1), a two-phase or
slower binding schedule for (2).
