Sweep 033 -- does a trained model survive having a new sense attached?
=======================================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Imagine the network has learned to do something, and then you plug in a new
sensor -- a channel that did not exist when it was trained. Most artificial
networks cannot take this: the input layer is a fixed-size matrix and widening
it means rebuilding and retraining from scratch. Brains manage it routinely.

This architecture is built so that it should be possible, because nothing is
indexed by input -- every connection names its source by number, so a new source
is just a new number. That already works on an *untrained* network.

The part nobody has measured is whether a network that has actually learned
something still knows it afterwards, and there is a specific reason to doubt it:
making room for the new channels means **throwing away a quarter of the existing
connections**. If what the network learned lived in those, attaching a sensor
costs it.

A yes here means capability can be added to a running system without taking it
down, which is most of the argument for this design outliving a fixed-shape
network. A no means growth is a thing you do to a blank network only, and the
claim needs re-stating in much narrower terms.

`Column.add_inputs(n_new, rewire_frac)` moves `rewire_frac` of every branch's
synapses onto the new channels -- fan-in is fixed at `(N, B, S)`, so growth is
rewiring rather than accretion. At the default that is **25% of every neuron's
fan-in reassigned, with the weights on those synapses re-drawn from the initial
distribution rather than inherited.**

Everything measured so far is on an untrained column: sparsity re-settles
0.0344 -> 0.0303 on its own, and both old and new channels drive the output
(153.9 and 59.8 against 0.0 with everything silent). None of that is a claim
about a learned function.

THE TWO COSTS, AND WHY EACH NEEDS ITS OWN CONDITION
------------------------------------------------------
A drop after growth has three possible causes, and they call for different
responses:

  damage        25% of the learned fan-in is destroyed and re-drawn. Intrinsic
                to fixed fan-in; would be paid even by a perfect sensor.

  distraction   the new channels inject uninformative drive into the branches
                that now read them.

  a harder task eight extra noise channels make delayed XOR harder *whatever*
                happens to the model. This one is not about the model at all,
                and conflating it with the other two would overstate the damage.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding on, 300 train + 100 recover)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  nogrow         no growth, same episode budget          drift baseline
  grow-silent    +8 channels fed zeros, rewire 0.25      **damage alone**
  grow-noise     +8 channels carrying noise, rewire 0.25 damage + distraction
  grow-noise-05  +8 channels carrying noise, rewire 0.05 does cost track the
                                                         rewire fraction?
  fresh-noise    built at the widened width and trained  **the task cost alone**
                 from scratch for the whole budget

`grow-silent` is what separates damage from distraction: the channels are added
and rewired to identically, but fed zeros, so the new synapses contribute
nothing and the only cost left is the destroyed ones.

`fresh-noise` is what separates both from the task getting harder. It has paid
the task cost and none of the damage, so it is the level growth could aspire to
rather than a before/after comparison -- its `cost` and `residual` columns are
reported as 0 by construction and mean nothing.

`nogrow` is not decorative. The `after` measurement is taken later in training
than `before`, and a frozen column drifts when anything about its input changes
-- sweep 013 lost a whole run to exactly that. Without `nogrow`, drift and damage
are the same number.

WHAT IS MEASURED
----------------
Linear decodability of the column state at three points: `before` growth,
`after` it with no recovery, and `recovered` after 100 further episodes.

  cost      = after - before        the immediate hit
  residual  = recovered - before    what is still missing once it has re-settled

Decodability rather than end-to-end accuracy, because the readout reads *neurons*
and growth does not touch it -- so an end-to-end number would understate column
damage. Sweep 009 is the standing warning that a readout can hide a column-level
difference entirely.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
--------------------------------------
  nogrow          cost   0.00 +/- 0.01     residual  0.00 +/- 0.02
  grow-silent     cost  -0.03 to -0.10     residual -0.01 to -0.05
  grow-noise      cost  -0.05 to -0.13     residual -0.02 to -0.07
  grow-noise-05   cost  -0.01 to -0.04     residual  0.00 to -0.02
  fresh-noise     level within 0.03 of `nogrow`'s `before`

**The central claim: the immediate cost is real and measurable, and the residual
after recovery is much smaller than it -- i.e. the model is damaged and then
mostly recovers.** Concretely, `grow-noise`'s residual should be under half its
cost.

**And the supporting claim, which is what makes it mechanism rather than
anecdote: the cost tracks `rewire_frac`.** `grow-noise-05` rewires a fifth as
many synapses and should cost roughly a fifth as much. If the cost is flat in
`rewire_frac`, the damage is not coming from the destroyed synapses and the
decomposition above is wrong.

The reasoning: sweep 022 established that most of a column's decodability is its
homeostatic operating point rather than anything learned, and homeostasis is
exactly what recovers on its own. Binding's own contribution is +0.074, so even
losing all of it caps the residual near that. A quarter of the fan-in re-drawn
should cost less than all of binding, because scaling and homeostasis re-settle
the rest.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS
-------------------------
  cost real, residual   Growth is survivable on a trained model. The honest
  small                 headline is "attaching a sensor costs X and recovers to
                        Y in 100 episodes", with both numbers quoted -- not
                        "growth is free", which is what the untrained-column
                        result would tempt someone into writing.

  residual ~ cost       The learned function is genuinely destroyed and does not
                        come back. Then `add_inputs` is a blank-network
                        operation, TODO item 5 and the README both need
                        re-stating, and the next question is whether inheriting
                        the displaced weights instead of re-drawing them helps.

  cost flat in          The damage is not from the destroyed synapses. Most
  rewire_frac           likely it is the drive change, and the fix is a
                        different one -- rewire less, or scale the new synapses
                        in gradually.

  grow-silent ~         Distraction costs nothing and the whole cost is damage.
  grow-noise            Good news for a real sensor, which would carry signal
                        rather than noise.

THE OUTCOME MOST LIKELY TO BE MISREAD
---------------------------------------
`grow-noise` scoring below `fresh-noise` would be tempting to report as damage,
and it is only damage *relative to a ceiling that also moved*. The comparison
that means "the model was hurt" is `grow-* vs nogrow`; the comparison that means
"growth got you close to where you could have been" is `grow-noise vs
fresh-noise`. They are different claims and the write-up must not merge them.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TEST
-------------------------
A sensor carrying *useful* information. Every new channel here is noise or
silence, so nothing measures whether the column can come to exploit a new sense
-- only whether it survives acquiring one. That is the more interesting question
and needs a task whose label depends on the new channels, which delayed XOR
cannot provide without becoming a different benchmark.

Also untested: repeated growth. One graft is not a stream of them, and the
damage may or may not accumulate.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding on, 300 training episodes then 100 recovery,
500 collection episodes per measurement, 8 new channels, noise rate 0.02 on the
new channels matching the task's own background rate.

The harness asserts in-run that the graft landed -- that some synapse points at
an appended source, and that driving the appended channels changes the column's
output. Both were verified to fail when `add_inputs` is broken so that no
synapse moves. Without them a failed graft would report that growth costs
nothing, which is the most reassuring possible outcome and a measurement of
nothing at all.
