Sweep 031 -- what is lateral inhibition actually doing?
========================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Two mechanisms in this project help. One of them, lateral inhibition, was added
for a reason that turned out to be wrong.

The idea was that making neurons which fire together inhibit each other would
spread information out, so different neurons end up carrying different things.
Sweep 020 measured that directly and it does not happen. The spreading is not
there.

But the mechanism still helps, by a decent margin, and nobody knows why. That is
an uncomfortable thing to have in the code -- a rule that works for unknown
reasons cannot be improved on purpose, cannot be predicted to survive a change
of task, and cannot be explained to anyone. The README currently says outright
that what it does is unmeasured and that no explanation should be written into
the code until one exists.

This is the attempt to produce one. A yes means we know which property of the
representation to aim at, and can ask whether other mechanisms hit it more
cheaply. A no narrows the field by eliminating the most plausible remaining
explanation, and the question becomes what is left.

THE HYPOTHESIS, AND WHY IT IS THE ONE LEFT
--------------------------------------------
If the representation's *shape* is unchanged but its *readability* improves, the
thing that changed is where the label sits inside that shape.

A linear decoder fitted to finite data resolves a representation's leading
directions well and its trailing directions badly -- that is the whole content
of sweep 028's `48-thin` control, and of the 0.802-vs-0.853 gap between 500 and
1200 collection episodes. So a mechanism that *rotates the label into the
leading directions* would raise linear decodability by a lot while leaving the
eigenvalue spectrum, the mean off-diagonal correlation and the firing rate all
exactly where they were.

That is the prediction: **lateral inhibition improves alignment between the
class-difference direction and the state's leading directions, without improving
class separability itself.**

It also makes a sharp negative prediction, which is what makes it worth running:
if alignment is the mechanism, then **Fisher separability should not improve** —
the label is no easier to separate in principle, only easier to *find*. If
separability rises instead, the representation genuinely got better and
alignment is not the story.

HONESTY ABOUT ORDERING
------------------------
The hypothesis and the three metrics were written into `experiments/geometry.py`
before it was run for the first time, and the ground-truth test
(`test_alignment_probe_recovers_a_known_label_direction`) was written and passing
before any column was measured. This note was written after the measurement runs
had been *launched* but before any number from them was read.

That is weaker than the usual standard, where the note precedes the run
outright, and it is recorded rather than glossed. The prediction below is not
retrofitted -- it is the docstring's, transcribed -- but the ordering is not
what rule 4 asks for and the next one should not copy it.

WHAT IS MEASURED  (experiments/geometry.py)
---------------------------------------------
None of these fits a decoder, deliberately. `binding.py` already reports a
decoder score, and sweep 009 established a decoder can hide a column-level
difference entirely. Everything here comes from class-conditional means and
covariances directly.

  align5 / align10   Fraction of the label direction `mu_1 - mu_0` captured by
                     the top 5 / top 10 eigenvectors of the state's correlation
                     matrix. **The hypothesis.**

  align_rand         The chance level, `sqrt(k/n)` = 0.323 at n = 96, k = 10.
                     Reported alongside rather than subtracted, because an
                     alignment of 0.4 sounds meaningful and is not.

  fisher             `|mu_1 - mu_0|^2 / tr(within-class covariance)`.
                     Separability with the decoder taken out of it. **The
                     negative prediction.**

  margin             Separation along the Fisher discriminant `S_w^-1 delta`,
                     ridge 1e-3.

Correlation rather than covariance throughout, because `probe.logistic`
standardises its inputs and the metric exists to explain what that decoder sees.

CONDITIONS  (frozen column, lr 0, 300 episodes, 500 collection, 96 neurons)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  off           neither mechanism
  lateral       lateral inhibition only     -- the condition under test
  on            binding only                -- the comparison
  lateral+on    both                        -- does it stack the same way?

`on` is here because binding's effect on this metric is *not* predicted to be
alignment: sweep 020 measured binding halving effective rank and raising
correlation, so binding changes the shape and lateral does not. If both come
back looking identical on these metrics, the metrics are not resolving the
difference and the sweep says so rather than being interpreted.

PREDICTION (transcribed from the probe's docstring)
------------------------------------------------------
  align5   lateral > off by a clear margin, both above the 0.323 chance level
  fisher   lateral ~= off, i.e. **no improvement in separability**
  on       align rises too, but accompanied by a large eff_rank drop, which is
           a different mechanism reaching a similar place

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS
-------------------------
  alignment up,        The explanation. Lateral inhibition is a *decoder-facing*
  fisher flat          mechanism: it does not make the column better, it makes
                       the column's existing information easier to reach with a
                       linear readout on finite data. That predicts the gain
                       shrinks as the decoder gets more data -- directly
                       testable against sweep 028's collection-budget effect,
                       and the first falsifiable consequence this mechanism has
                       ever had.

  alignment flat,      The representation genuinely improved and the improvement
  fisher up            is invisible to correlation, rank and sparsity alike.
                       Then the question is what the extra separation is made
                       of, and the metrics here are not enough.

  both up              Two things happening at once; needs the collection-budget
                       test to separate them.

  both flat            The most likely outcome and the least satisfying: +0.028
                       comes from something none of these five quantities sees.
                       The direction is then genuinely open, and the honest
                       response is to say so in the README rather than to keep
                       proposing explanations.

The last one is the one to be careful not to explain away. Four structural
metrics have already come back unchanged for this mechanism; a fifth and sixth
doing the same is a real possibility and not a failure of the sweep.

THE PROBE IS NOT BLIND, WHICH IS THE FIRST THING TO ESTABLISH
---------------------------------------------------------------
Binding comes back significant on two metrics at the smallest p six seeds can
produce, and its effective rank drops 8.0 on 0/6 seeds. So these quantities do
resolve a mechanism when one is acting on them. A null for lateral inhibition is
therefore about lateral inhibition rather than about the instrument.

`lateral`'s effective rank moves -0.254 at p = 0.4375, which independently
reproduces sweep 020's null on the same quantity. The setup is measuring the
thing it measured before.

SIX SEEDS CANNOT ANSWER THIS, AND SAYING "NULL" WOULD BE WRONG
----------------------------------------------------------------
An exact paired permutation test over 6 seeds has a **minimum achievable p of
2/2^6 = 0.031**. So `p = 0.0625` is the second-smallest value obtainable -- it
means 5 of 6 seeds moved the same way and nothing more. Two metrics, `align10`
and `fisher`, both sit exactly there.

The effect being chased is +0.028 in decodability at p = 0.0297 over 20 seeds.
A structural signature of an effect that small needs at least as many seeds as
the effect itself did. **Six seeds is a pilot, and this sweep's conclusion is
that it must be run at twenty**, not that lateral inhibition does nothing to
these quantities.

WHERE THE PREDICTION STANDS
-----------------------------
Predicted: alignment up, Fisher separability flat. What the pilot shows, with no
significance behind any of it:

  align5    -0.020    the wrong direction
  align10   +0.036    the predicted direction, 5/6
  fisher    +0.005    the direction predicted NOT to move, 5/6

So the pilot points, weakly, at the *alternative* branch this note named --
"the representation genuinely improved and the improvement is invisible to
correlation, rank and sparsity alike" -- rather than at the alignment
hypothesis. It also splits `align5` from `align10`, which the prediction treated
as one quantity and which may not be.

**No conclusion is drawn from that.** A one-seed claim has been corrected by
twenty seeds three times in this project, and a six-seed claim in a direction
opposite to the prediction is exactly the shape of thing that reverses. It is
recorded because the prediction was recorded, and because deciding what to run
at twenty seeds is what the pilot is for.
