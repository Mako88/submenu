Sweep 018 -- can the gain arrive without stopping the world?
============================================================

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 017 established that salience-gated Hebbian binding pays end to end, but
only once binding stops:

  flat, delta readout            +0.007   p = 0.68
  RLS readout                    +0.043   p = 0.0063
  binding frozen, readout catches up  +0.063   p = 0.0013

and that the readout does not need more time in general -- catchup-off scored
0.707 against e2e-off's 0.709. It needs a column that has stopped moving.

That is a real limitation stated plainly: a system that must stop learning
before its readout can use what it learned has deferred continual learning, not
solved it. This asks whether the gain is available *within the same episode
budget*, without a special phase bolted on.

The catchup condition in 017 used 450 episodes against everyone else's 300, so
it was never a fair comparison -- it showed the gain exists, not that it is
free. Every condition here runs exactly 300.

CONDITIONS  (all 300 episodes, 20 seeds, 96 neurons, column lr 0)
------------------------------------------------------------------
  off            no binding
  flat           binding at a constant rate               (= 017's e2e-on)
  decay-0.99     hebb_decay 0.99   -> rate x0.05 by episode 300
  decay-0.98     hebb_decay 0.98   -> rate x0.002, effectively off by ~200
  phase          bind for 150 episodes, then readout only for 150

`phase` is the direct test: same budget, binding simply stops halfway.
`decay` is the softer version -- does a *slowing* representation suffice, or
does the readout need it genuinely static?

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
------------------------------------
  phase        0.750 to 0.780.  High confidence it beats flat significantly.
               017's catchup reached 0.770 with 150 extra episodes; the same
               150 static episodes taken out of the binding budget should land
               close, because binding does most of its work early -- the
               baseline it scores against converges, so late events contribute
               progressively less.

  decay-0.98   0.740 to 0.775, probably significant. Close to `phase` in
               effect, since a rate at 0.2% of its initial value is a stopped
               column in all but name.

  decay-0.99   0.725 to 0.760, and this is the one I would bet against. At
               episode 300 the rate is still 5% of initial, and 017 says the
               readout wants static, not slow.

Stated sharply so it can be wrong: **if decay-0.99 matches phase, "slow enough"
is sufficient and the mechanism is fine as a continuous rule. If only
decay-0.98 and phase work, the requirement is genuinely a stop, and this is a
two-phase learner that should be described as one.**

The outcome I would find most interesting is `phase` >= 0.770 on 300 episodes,
because that would mean the 150 episodes of binding thrown away cost nothing --
the mechanism saturates early and the rest of the budget is better spent on the
readout. That is a schedule finding with an obvious next question: how few
binding episodes are actually needed?

FAILURE RESPONSE, NAMED IN ADVANCE
-----------------------------------
If every scheduled condition is null and only 017's extra-budget catchup works,
then the gain is not free and the honest description is that binding needs a
consolidation phase the current architecture has no way to schedule for itself.
That is not a defeat -- it is a specific, structural finding about what the
mechanism requires, and it would make "when does a column decide it has learned
enough" the next question rather than another hyperparameter.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds
-------------------------
condition    episodes            accuracy   vs off    p
off          300, no binding     0.709
flat         300 bind            0.715      +0.007   0.6834
decay-99     300 bind, x0.99/ev  0.723      +0.014   0.2159
decay-98     300 bind, x0.98/ev  0.715      +0.006   0.5593
phase        150 bind +150 idle  0.731      +0.023   0.1008
catchup      300 bind +150 idle  0.770      +0.063   0.0013   (sweep 017)

  phase vs decay-98: +0.016, 13/20, p = 0.2322.  No difference.

**Every condition inside the 300-episode budget is null.** Only the 450-episode
catchup works. The gain is not free.

All three predictions were wrong. `phase` was predicted at 0.750-0.780 with
high confidence it would beat flat significantly; it reached 0.731 at p = 0.10.
`decay-98` was predicted 0.740-0.775 and probably significant; it reached 0.715,
which is flat's number exactly. `decay-99` was the one I bet against, and it
was null -- but so was everything else, so being right about it was not
insight.

WHAT THE NUMBERS ACTUALLY SAY
------------------------------
The prediction's named branch was "binding needs a consolidation phase the
architecture cannot schedule for itself". That is close but not quite what the
data shows, and the difference matters.

Line them up by what each condition spends:

  flat      300 bind,   0 idle  -> 0.715
  phase     150 bind, 150 idle  -> 0.731
  catchup   300 bind, 150 idle  -> 0.770

The idle period is worth something only on top of a *full* binding budget.
Taking 150 episodes of binding away to pay for it gives back almost all of the
gain. So the finding is not "binding must stop" -- it is that **binding needs
roughly 300 episodes to do its work, and the readout needs a static period
after that, and the two cannot be paid for out of one budget.**

That also explains why decay failed at both rates. A decaying schedule spends
its late episodes binding weakly, which is the worst of both: not enough
binding to finish, not static enough for the readout to settle.

A CONFOUND IN `phase`, RECORDED RATHER THAN GLOSSED
----------------------------------------------------
`phase` varies two things at once -- binding duration and idle duration -- so on
its own it cannot separate "the stop came too early" from "the binding was too
short". The comparison against catchup resolves it, since catchup holds binding
at 300 and only adds idle time, but that is an argument across sweeps rather
than within one. A cleaner design would have held binding fixed and varied only
the idle period. Noting it because the same mistake in sweep 013 cost a whole
run, and this time the cross-sweep comparison happens to rescue it.

NOT DOING THE OBVIOUS THING
----------------------------
The obvious move is to sweep bind-episodes across 200/250/300 and find where it
crosses significance. The prediction ruled that out in advance, and it stands:
after a null and two follow-ups, adding hyperparameters until one is
significant is how a project talks itself into a result. Twenty seeds and a
p-value do not protect against searching over conditions.

What is established is enough to act on: **binding buys about +0.06 end to end,
and costs about 50% more training episodes to collect.** That is a real,
quantified trade rather than a free lunch, and it should be described that way.

WHAT THIS MAKES INTERESTING
----------------------------
Why binding needs ~300 episodes at all. It writes into W on every modulator
release, and the baseline it scores against converges early, so the late
episodes contribute progressively less -- yet cutting them in half clearly
hurts. Something is still accumulating late that the diagnostics do not
currently measure.

That is a question about the mechanism rather than about a schedule, and it is
answerable directly: track linear decodability of the column *during* training
rather than only at the end, and see when the 0.802 -> 0.876 gain actually
lands. If it lands by episode 150, the problem is the readout and `phase`
should have worked. If it lands late, binding is genuinely slow and the next
question is why.

That measurement comes before any further schedule.
