Sweep 036 -- is heterogeneous membrane timing "a large win", as claimed?
========================================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Every neuron here forgets at its own rate. Some hold onto what they have seen
for a few milliseconds, some for a third of a second, and the spread is drawn
randomly when the network is built. The design document says this variety is one
of the important things about the model -- that a network where every neuron
forgets at the same speed simply could not hold a memory across a gap the way
this one does.

That has never been tested. It is one of the four headline departures from
biology in the design document, it is stated in strong terms, and there is no
measurement anywhere behind it.

There is also a specific reason to doubt it. The thing that actually carries
memory across the gap in this task was measured, and it is not the membrane at
all -- it is the synapses, which hold a trace of recent activity without any
ongoing firing. Turning that off collapses the task from 0.86 to 0.53. If the
synapses are doing the remembering, the variety of membrane speeds may be worth
much less than the document claims.

Either answer is useful. If the spread pays, it is a cheap design win worth
stating with a number. If it does not, a headline claim comes out of the design
document, and the honest description of the model gets simpler.

QUESTION
--------
`DESIGN.md`, section 2, in full:

  "Biology's are pinned by channel kinetics. Ours are parameters, spread
   log-uniformly over 12-320 ms. **The spread alone is a large win: a population
   with mixed constants holds working memory a homogeneous one cannot.**"

`README.md` carries the same claim in its deviations table. Nothing measures it.
`test_membrane_gain_is_independent_of_time_constant` is sometimes read as
covering this and does not: it guards the unit-DC-gain fix, which was a *bug the
spread exposed* -- long-tau neurons being louder rather than longer-memoried --
not the benefit the spread is supposed to deliver.

WHY THE OBVIOUS COMPARISON IS A STRAW MAN
--------------------------------------------
"Heterogeneous beats homogeneous" is easy to demonstrate badly. Pick one fixed
tau, show it loses, declare the spread essential. But a homogeneous population
at a *badly chosen* constant losing says nothing -- the claim is that no single
choice works, and that requires trying several.

So the homogeneous conditions span the range, including values chosen to be
plausible rather than convenient:

  homo-12    the short end of the spread
  homo-40    near the geometric mean of 12 and 320 (62); 40 is below it
  homo-100   matched to the task's 100-step cue spacing -- the value someone
             tuning by hand would pick
  homo-320   the long end

**The comparison that decides the claim is `hetero` against the BEST of these**,
not against their mean and not against any one of them.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding off, drain 0)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  hetero      tau_soma 12-320 log-uniform     the default; reproduces 0.802
  homo-12     every neuron at 12 ms
  homo-40     every neuron at 40 ms
  homo-100    every neuron at 100 ms
  homo-320    every neuron at 320 ms

Binding off throughout: this is a question about the substrate, and a plasticity
rule on top would only make it harder to read. `drain 0`, so `hetero` must
reproduce **0.802** or the sweep is not comparable to anything.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
--------------------------------------
  hetero      0.802   (established; anything else voids the sweep)
  homo-12     0.60 to 0.72
  homo-40     0.68 to 0.78
  homo-100    0.70 to 0.80
  homo-320    0.62 to 0.74

**The central claim: the spread helps, but by far less than "a large win", and
"cannot" is wrong. `hetero` beats the best homogeneous condition by +0.02 to
+0.08, not by the qualitative gap the design document implies.**

The reasoning is the STP measurement. Short-term plasticity is what bridges the
250-step delay -- 0.527 without it against 0.864 with it -- and that mechanism
lives in the synapse and is identical in every condition here. So the memory the
claim is about is *already provided by something else*, and the membrane spread
can only be adding basis diversity on top of it.

`homo-100` is predicted to be the best homogeneous condition, because a constant
matched to the cue spacing is the one a person tuning by hand would land on, and
the interval it has to bridge is exactly that.

**And the outcome that would refute my reasoning rather than the document's:**
if `homo-320` or `homo-12` wins among the homogeneous conditions, then the
useful thing about a membrane constant here is not matching the task's timescale
and I have the mechanism wrong even if the number comes out as predicted.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS
-------------------------
  hetero wins big       The document is right and now has a number. Worth
  (+0.15 or more)       stating plainly, and worth asking whether an even wider
                        or differently-shaped spread does better.

  hetero wins a         Predicted. The claim is directionally right and
  little (+0.02..+0.08) overstated. "A large win" and "cannot" both come out of
                        DESIGN.md and README.md and are replaced with the
                        measured margin. This is a correction to the record,
                        which rule 5 requires be made rather than softened.

  hetero ties the best  The spread buys nothing measurable on this task. Then it
  homogeneous           stays -- it costs nothing and biology has it -- but it
                        stops being described as load-bearing, and the four
                        headline departures become three.

  hetero loses          A single well-chosen constant is better. That would be
                        genuinely surprising, would make the default wrong, and
                        would need its own follow-up before anything is changed.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TEST
-------------------------
A task whose structure spans several timescales. Delayed XOR has one interval,
100 steps, so a single constant matched to it is unusually well placed here --
which biases the sweep **against** heterogeneity. That is the honest direction
of the bias and it is worth saying: if the spread wins even on the task most
favourable to a single constant, the claim is stronger than the number looks.

Nor does it test `lr_tau > 0`, where the constants are learned rather than
sampled. That path exists, is off by default, and is a separate question.

20 seeds, 300 episodes, 500 collection episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding and
lateral off, `drain_steps 0`, `tau_branch` and delays at their defaults.
Homogeneity is `tau_soma_min == tau_soma_max`.
