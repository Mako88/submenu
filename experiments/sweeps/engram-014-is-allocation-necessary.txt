Sweep 014 -- can the engram mechanism be deleted?
=================================================

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 012 found the first mechanism in this project to beat a frozen column:
alloc+hebb, linear 0.831 vs 0.802, +0.029 on 14/20 seeds, p = 0.0176. Local and
unsupervised, which is what makes it interesting.

Its own ablations then said the allocation machinery is not what earned it:

  alloc alone (no binding)   0.758, reliably WORSE than off, p = 0.0099
  no refractory              0.844, if anything better than the full mechanism
  excite/recruitment corr    -0.012 in the winning condition

So the obvious reading is that salience-gated Hebbian binding carries the whole
effect and the excitability drift, the recruitment competition and the
allocation refractory are scaffolding. This sweep tries to establish that,
because if it holds, most of what was built for sweep 012 should be deleted.

CONDITIONS
----------
  off          frozen column, the baseline to beat
  full         excitability + competition + refractory + tagged binding
  no-refract   as full, minus the allocation refractory
  tag-only     competition + tagged binding, no excitability drift, no
               refractory. Isolates the competition.
  hebb-only    bind_mode="graded". No competition, no excitability, no
               refractory, no tag. Every neuron binds in proportion to its own
               activity relative to its own baseline, gated on a modulator
               release. Plain Hebbian LTP and nothing else.

hebb-only is scale-matched to the tagged modes by construction -- the graded
activity ratio is multiplied by alloc_frac so the mean weight increment agrees,
measured at |dW| 0.0722 vs 0.0724 over 40 episodes. That matters: without it
the comparison would vary learning rate and mechanism together, which is
exactly the defect that made sweep 013 uninterpretable, and there is a test
asserting the match holds.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
------------------------------------
Primary, moderate-to-high confidence: **hebb-only matches full**, within
+/- 0.02 and with no significant paired difference. If so, the engram apparatus
comes out -- the drift, the margin adaptation, the refractory, the excitability
setpoint coupling and roughly 120 lines of column.py.

Named in advance so it cannot be softened afterwards: if hebb-only lands within
0.02 of full and p > 0.05, that is a deletion, not a "both work, keep both".

Secondary, lower confidence: tag-only sits between them, because the
competition concentrates binding on the neurons that responded most, and that
may sharpen the representation slightly even when nothing is allocated in a
memory sense.

Low confidence, and the one result that would save the mechanism: hebb-only
beats off but by clearly less than full, with the gap traceable to the
competition rather than to scale. That would mean recruitment is doing real
work that the 012 ablations were too coarse to see -- `alloc` alone removes
binding entirely, which is not the same as removing the competition.

What this sweep CANNOT show: whether allocation pays off on a task that asks
for memory separation. Delayed XOR asks nothing of it -- one episode, one
answer, no interference between memories -- so a null here is evidence that
allocation is useless *on this benchmark*, not in general. The refractory
demonstrably does the thing it was built for (rotate 0.815 against 1.661
without it, i.e. engrams overlapping less than independent sampling). If
hebb-only wins here, the honest move is to delete the machinery from the
default path and keep the finding recorded, rather than to keep dead code
warm on the strength of a benchmark that was never asked the question.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, 500 collection episodes, column lr 0
throughout so the three-factor rule cannot contribute. Identical to sweep 012
in every respect, so 012's numbers are directly comparable.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds
-------------------------
condition      linear
off             0.802 +/- 0.051
full            0.830 +/- 0.049
tag-only        0.871 +/- 0.041
hebb-only       0.876 +/- 0.045

  full       vs off         +0.028, 14/20 seeds, p = 0.0203   significant
  full       vs hebb-only   -0.047,  1/20 seeds, p = 0.0001   significant, negative
  tag-only   vs hebb-only   -0.005,  6/20 seeds, p = 0.52     nothing

The prediction was WRONG, and usefully so. It said hebb-only would *match*
full within +/- 0.02 at p > 0.05. hebb-only does not match full; it beats it on
19 of 20 seeds at p = 0.0001, and beats a frozen column by +0.074 -- more than
two and a half times what the full mechanism managed.

A tie would have left "both work, keep both" arguable. This does not.

WHERE THE HARM IS
-----------------
The ladder localises it, because each rung removes exactly one thing:

  full -> tag-only    +0.041   excitability drift, gain, and the allocation
                               refractory. This is the cost.
  tag-only -> hebb-only +0.005 the recruitment competition. Nothing, either way.

So the allocation machinery is not neutral scaffolding around a working
mechanism. It is a liability, and specifically the excitability half of it. The
recruitment competition is merely inert. Salience-gated Hebbian LTP -- the
simplest component, present in the mechanism from the start -- is the entire
effect, and the apparatus built around it was subtracting from it.

Worth stating plainly, since sweep 012 read as a success: 012's +0.029 was real
and replicates here at +0.028. It was also less than half of what was available
from the part of the mechanism nobody had isolated. A significant positive
result is not evidence that the mechanism producing it is the right one.

WHY THE MACHINERY HURTS -- hypothesis, unmeasured
--------------------------------------------------
Tagged binding writes a full-strength update into ~17% of neurons and nothing
into the rest, so each episode's structure is written into a small, largely
arbitrary subset. Graded binding writes into every neuron in proportion to how
much it actually responded. Under synaptic scaling, which renormalises each
branch to a fixed budget, the graded version is a smooth reallocation and the
tagged version is a repeated shock to a changing minority. The excitability
drift makes the choice of minority partly random, which would explain why
removing it recovers most of the gap.

That is a story, not a measurement, and it is recorded as such. It would be
tested by tagging deterministically on activity rank -- but see below.

ACTION TAKEN
------------
The deletion the prediction committed to, carried out: excitability drift, the
homeostatic setpoint coupling, the recruitment competition and its margin
adaptation, the allocation refractory, and the tagged binding path are removed
from plexus/column.py. What remains is `hebbian`: on a modulator release, every
neuron strengthens the excitatory synapses that were driving it, in proportion
to its own activity against its own baseline. Off by default, as every new
mechanism is.

The allocation results are NOT deleted -- they are here, and they include a
real one: the refractory does produce engrams that overlap less than
independent sampling (rotate 0.815 against 1.661 without it). Delayed XOR has
one episode, one answer and no interference between memories, so it never asks
for that. If a capacity or continual-learning benchmark ever does, this file
and the git history are where the mechanism is, and it should be rebuilt from
the measurement rather than from memory of it.

NEXT
----
End-to-end confirmation of `hebbian` against a frozen column. The effect to
confirm is now +0.074 rather than +0.029, which is a much easier target -- and
sweep 009 is the standing warning that representation quality and accuracy can
still come apart.
