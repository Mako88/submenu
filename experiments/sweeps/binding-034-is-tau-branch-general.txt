Sweep 034 -- is `tau_branch` a real improvement, or compensation for a tail?
============================================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
Sweep 030 went looking for a way to tolerate a late learning signal, and its
*control condition* -- the boring one, there only so the interesting numbers had
something to be compared against -- produced the largest single effect anyone has
measured in this project. Turning up one filter, which controls how long each
dendritic branch remembers its input, was worth more than every learning rule
combined and more than the homeostatic tuning that was previously the biggest
thing here.

That is either the most useful accident so far or a mirage, and which one it is
depends on a detail. Every condition in that sweep ran with a long silent gap
tacked onto the end of each episode -- 200 steps of nothing, needed so that a
late signal has somewhere to arrive. A branch that remembers for 15 steps forgets
everything across a gap that long. One that remembers for 120 steps does not.

So the improvement may be real, or it may be the filter merely surviving a
handicap that the standard setup does not impose. This sweep removes the gap and
looks again. It is the difference between "we found a much better setting" and
"we found a workaround for a problem we introduced".

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 030, 20 paired seeds, binding off, `drain_steps 200` throughout:

    tau_branch 15      0.620
    tau_branch 60      0.838     +0.218   20/20   p = 0.0000
    tau_branch 120     0.918     +0.298   20/20   p = 0.0000

For scale: homeostatic settling is worth +0.197 (sweep 022), salience-gated
binding +0.074 (sweeps 015-025), lateral inhibition +0.028 (sweep 020), and the
designed three-factor rule -0.003 (sweeps 006-010). **A forward-path constant
nobody has ever tuned is worth more than all of them together.**

THE CONFOUND, WHICH IS THE ENTIRE REASON FOR THIS SWEEP
---------------------------------------------------------
`drain_steps 200` appends 200 silent steps to every episode. It exists so a
modulator arriving 150 steps late still lands inside the run, and sweep 026
pinned it across all conditions so that lag comparisons are not secretly tail
comparisons.

But the *baseline* it produces is 0.620, against the **0.802** that sweeps
015-025 measure with no tail. So the tail costs 0.182 on its own, and
`tau_branch 120` recovers 0.298 of decodability in a regime that had 0.182
artificially removed. Two readings fit every number in sweep 030:

  general      a longer branch filter is simply better, and the standard
               `tau_branch = 15` has been leaving 0.2-0.3 of decodability on the
               table since the project began.

  compensatory a longer branch filter forgets less across 200 silent steps, so
               it recovers ground the tail took away and does nothing once the
               tail is gone.

These imply completely different actions -- change the default, versus change
nothing -- and sweep 030 cannot tell them apart, because it has no `drain 0`
condition at any `tau_branch` other than the implicit standing one.

**This is the same shape as the error sweep 026 already paid for**: a parameter
measured in a regime whose baseline had moved, reported as though the baseline
had not. Recording that explicitly, because the temptation to quote +0.298 is
strong and the number is currently about a configuration rather than about the
model.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding off)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
  tb15-drain0     tau_branch 15,  drain 0     reproduces the standing 0.802
  tb30-drain0     tau_branch 30,  drain 0
  tb60-drain0     tau_branch 60,  drain 0     **the sweep**
  tb120-drain0    tau_branch 120, drain 0
  tb240-drain0    tau_branch 240, drain 0     past the cue spacing; should fail

  tb15-drain200   tau_branch 15,  drain 200   reproduces sweep 030's 0.620
  tb120-drain200  tau_branch 120, drain 200   reproduces sweep 030's 0.918

The last two are there so this sweep contains its own link to sweep 030 rather
than relying on numbers from another run. If they do not reproduce, something
else changed and nothing here is comparable.

`tb240` is included because it should be bad, and a condition predicted to fail
is worth more than four predicted to succeed. The task's cues are 100 steps
apart with a 25-step burst; a filter with a 240-step memory cannot separate cue
one from cue two, so decodability should fall. If it does *not* fall, the filter
is not doing what its name says and the whole interpretation is wrong.

Binding is off in every condition. This sweep is about the forward path alone,
and adding a plasticity rule would only make it harder to read.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
--------------------------------------
  tb15-drain0     0.802     (established; anything else voids the sweep)
  tb30-drain0     0.80 to 0.86
  tb60-drain0     0.82 to 0.90
  tb120-drain0    0.78 to 0.88
  tb240-drain0    0.55 to 0.72     -- should be clearly worse
  tb15-drain200   0.620     (reproduces sweep 030)
  tb120-drain200  0.918     (reproduces sweep 030)

**The central claim: `tau_branch` helps at `drain 0` too, but by much less than
+0.298 -- somewhere between +0.02 and +0.10 at its best setting, rather than
+0.20 to +0.30.** So the effect is predicted to be *partly* general and *mostly*
compensatory.

The reasoning: the task holds cues 100 steps apart, so integrating over a longer
window genuinely should help hold the first cue until the second arrives, and 15
steps is plainly short for that. But most of sweep 030's effect has to be tail
compensation, because the tail alone costs 0.182 and a filter that spans it
recovers most of what it took.

**And the shape matters as much as the size: with the tail gone the optimum
should be interior** -- best somewhere around 30-60, falling by 240 -- whereas
sweep 030 is monotone increasing across every setting it tried. A monotone curve
at `drain 0` too would mean the filter is not trading anything off, which would
be surprising and would need explaining rather than celebrating.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS
-------------------------
  helps at drain 0     A real and large finding, and an embarrassing one: the
  by +0.20 or more     default has been wrong since the project started and every
                       number in the repo was measured with a badly-set forward
                       path. Everything would need re-running at the better
                       setting before any of it can be compared.

  helps by +0.02       Predicted. `tau_branch` is worth tuning, the default moves
  to +0.10             modestly, and sweep 030's +0.298 gets re-described as
                       mostly tail compensation. The latency result from 030 is
                       untouched either way, because it was measured against a
                       matched reference at the same `tau_branch`.

  no effect at         Purely compensatory. Then sweep 030's control number is a
  drain 0              fact about `drain_steps` and not about the column, the
                       README paragraph gets rewritten, and the interesting
                       remaining question is why 200 silent steps cost 0.182 at
                       all.

  hurts at drain 0     The standard setting is already near-optimal and the
                       original cost prediction in sweep 030 was right after all,
                       just measured in a regime that inverted it.

  tb240 does not       The branch filter is not integrating over the window its
  degrade              constant says. Distrust every reading above and check the
                       filter directly before interpreting anything.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TEST
-------------------------
Whether `tau_branch` interacts with the mechanisms. Binding is off throughout, so
if a longer filter changes what binding can do -- and sweep 030 hints it might,
since binding's gain shrank from +0.082 at `tau_branch 60` to +0.011 at 120 as
its reference approached ceiling -- that is a separate matrix.

Nor does it revisit the latency result. Sweep 030's lag conditions were each
measured against a reference at their own `tau_branch`, so whatever this sweep
finds about the general level, **+0.062 at lag 25 and +0.037 at lag 50 stand**.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, 500 collection episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding and
lateral off. `drain_steps` 0 or 200 as stated, `tau_act_fast` at its default 50
throughout since sweep 026 established it is not the lever.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds
-------------------------
  condition        linear     vs tb15-drain0
  tb15-drain0       0.802     --            (equals `off` exactly, p = 1.0000)
  tb30-drain0       0.852     +0.050  17/20  p = 0.0002
  tb60-drain0       0.858     +0.056  18/20  p = 0.0004   <- best
  tb120-drain0      0.843     +0.041  14/20  p = 0.0365
  tb240-drain0      0.822     +0.020  13/20  p = 0.2351   null

  tb15-drain200     0.620     reproduces sweep 030
  tb120-drain200    0.918     +0.298  20/20  p = 0.0000   reproduces 030 exactly

THE CENTRAL CLAIM HELD, AND +0.298 WAS MOSTLY THE TAIL
--------------------------------------------------------
Predicted: `tau_branch` helps at `drain 0` too, but by **+0.02 to +0.10** at its
best setting rather than the +0.20 to +0.30 sweep 030 reported.

Measured: **+0.056** at `tau_branch 60`, on 18 of 20 seeds at p = 0.0004.

So of sweep 030's +0.298, roughly **+0.056 is a real general improvement and
+0.242 is compensation for the 200-step drain tail** -- a 15-step filter forgets
across 200 silent steps and a 120-step one does not. Four fifths of the largest
single-parameter effect ever measured in this project was an artefact of the
regime it was measured in.

That is exactly what the AUDIT entry written at the time said it might be, and
why +0.298 was recorded there as "a result about a configuration" rather than
about the model. The guard worked.

**Both link conditions reproduce sweep 030 exactly** -- 0.620 and 0.918, and the
same +0.298 at 20/20 -- so this is a re-scoping of that result, not a
contradiction of it. And `tb15-drain0` equals the standing `off` to +0.000 on
0/20 seeds at p = 1.0000, so the sweep is comparable to everything else.

THE SHAPE CLAIM HELD TOO
--------------------------
Predicted: with the tail gone the optimum should be **interior** -- best around
30-60, falling by 240 -- where sweep 030 was monotone increasing across every
setting it tried.

Measured: 0.802 -> 0.852 -> **0.858** -> 0.843 -> 0.822. Interior optimum at 60,
declining through 120 and 240. The monotone curve in 030 was the tail
compensation growing, not the filter improving without limit.

WHERE THE PREDICTION WAS WRONG, AND IT WAS THE CONDITION THAT COULD FAIL
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
  predicted  tb240-drain0  **0.55 to 0.72, clearly worse**
  measured                 **0.822, +0.020 at p = 0.2351 -- null, if anything
                           slightly better than baseline**

`tb240` was included precisely because it was predicted to fail: "a condition
predicted to fail is worth more than four predicted to succeed". It did not
fail. The reasoning was that the task's cues are 100 steps apart and a 240-step
memory cannot separate them.

**And this note pre-committed to what to do about that:** "if it does not
degrade then the filter is not doing what its name says. Distrust every reading
above and check the filter directly before interpreting anything."

So the filter was checked directly. Driving one impulse into a column with
recurrence silenced and fitting the decay of the branch potential:

    configured tau_branch    15      60     240
    measured  tau_branch    15.0    60.0   240.0

The filter is exactly what it claims to be. **The pre-commitment fires on my
reasoning, not on the mechanism**, and the readings above stand.

(The first attempt at that check was contaminated and is worth recording: with
recurrence live, the branch potential *grows* after an impulse -- measured decay
above 1.0 -- because the column's own output feeds back into its branches. It
had to be silenced to measure the filter at all.)

Why the reasoning was wrong is the interesting part. It assumed the branch
potential is what carries cue identity across the gap. **It is not -- short-term
plasticity is**, which is the largest single measured effect in the model
(0.527 -> 0.864) and lives in synaptic efficacy rather than in any filter. So
smearing the branch filter over 240 steps does not erase which cue occurred,
because that information was never in the branch potential to begin with. A
long filter costs a little precision and no memory.

That is a testable consequence rather than a story: with STP disabled,
`tb240-drain0` **should** collapse, because then the branch filter would be
carrying the memory. Nothing here tests it.

WHAT THIS CHANGES, AND WHAT IT DELIBERATELY DOES NOT
------------------------------------------------------
`tau_branch = 60` is worth +0.056 at p = 0.0004 over the shipped 15, from a
forward-path constant nobody has ever tuned. That is comparable to salience-gated
binding's +0.074 and larger than lateral inhibition's +0.028 -- both of which are
mechanisms with sweeps behind them.

**The default is not being changed, and the reason belongs on the record.**
Every standing number in this project -- the 0.802 baseline, binding's +0.074,
lateral's +0.028, the capacity curve, the operating-point results -- was measured
at `tau_branch = 15`. Moving the default would invalidate all of them as a
comparison set for no gain that could not be had by re-baselining deliberately.
Rule 11 applies to a parameter change exactly as it does to a bug fix: the fix
does not only correct the future.

So this is recorded as **a known better setting that the defaults do not use**,
and re-baselining at 60 is a decision to take on purpose, with the sweeps it
would invalidate listed first.
