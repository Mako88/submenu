Sweep 030 -- can the latency window be bought, and what does it cost?
=====================================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
The whole reason for building this architecture is that it should run spread
across ordinary machines on the internet. Sweep 026 found the catch: the signal
that drives the one learning mechanism that works has to arrive within about 12
steps, or there is nothing left for it to act on. Twelve steps is roughly twelve
milliseconds. That is a room, not a planet.

That sweep also found out *why*, and the reason points at a specific dial. The
rule multiplies two fading signals together, and the faster of the two -- a
filter in the part of the column that does the ordinary work of processing
input, nothing to do with learning -- is what sets the deadline. Turning that
dial up widens the deadline; the probe measured it going from 12 steps to 29.

So the question is whether that trade is worth taking. Widening a filter in the
processing path means the column processes differently. It might get worse at
the task, and if it gets much worse then buying latency tolerance costs more
than it buys.

There is a second way, added while this note was being written, and it is the
better one if it works. Rather than stretching a filter the column needs for its
day job, give the learning rule *its own* memory of what the inputs were doing,
on whatever timescale it likes. The processing stays exactly as it was -- that
is checked, not assumed -- and the deadline goes from 12 steps to 154. That is
long enough to cross an ocean.

This sweep runs both, side by side, and prices them. What we want to learn is
whether either version still learns as well when the signal is on time, and
whether the second one still learns when the signal is 150 steps late. A yes
means distributed learning is a setting we choose. A no on both means the
mechanism is stuck in one building and something other than a time constant has
to change.

One thing to be clear about either way: this is about whether learning *survives*
the delay, not about whether it becomes better learning. Nothing here makes the
mechanism stronger.

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 026 established, at 20 seeds:

  binding is worth +0.127 at modulator lag 0 (19/20, p = 0.0000)
  and null from lag 25 onward (+0.016, p = 0.1610)

and `experiments/lagwindow.py` established why, by measuring the size of the
weight update binding commits as a function of lag. `_bind` commits a product of
two traces:

    dW  ~  post x pre
    post = act_fast / baseline    tau_act_fast = 50
    pre  = the branch filter      tau_branch   = 15

A product decays at the sum of the rates, so the window is
`1/(1/50 + 1/15) = 11.5` steps and is dominated by the shorter constant.
Measured against three settings, tail fits:

    tau_act_fast   tau_branch    predicted    measured
         50            15          11.54       11.87
        250            15          14.15       15.25
         50            60          27.27       28.68

**`tau_branch` is the lever, and it is not a plasticity setting.** It is the
branch filter in the forward path -- how the column integrates its input in the
first place. So this sweep is not "tune a learning constant"; it is "does the
column still work when you change how it computes, and is the latency you get
back worth it".

THE TWO HALVES, WHICH HAVE TO BE MEASURED SEPARATELY
------------------------------------------------------
  the cost      does a column with tau_branch 60 decode delayed XOR at all?
                Measured with binding OFF, at lag 0, against the standing
                tau_branch 15 baseline. Nothing to do with latency.

  the purchase  does binding's gain at lag 25 and lag 50 come back?
                Measured against the *matched* off-condition at the same
                tau_branch, never against the tau_branch 15 baseline -- or the
                comparison would mix the cost and the purchase into one number
                and neither would be readable.

Sweep 026's first run failed for exactly the matched-reference reason, so every
`on` condition here has an `off` condition at its own settings.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, drain pinned at 200)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
  off-tb15-lag0     off, tau_branch 15    the standing reference, 0.620
  on-tb15-lag0      on,  tau_branch 15    the standing +0.127
  on-tb15-lag25     on,  tau_branch 15    the standing null, +0.016

  off-tb60-lag0     off, tau_branch 60    **the cost**
  on-tb60-lag0      on,  tau_branch 60    does binding still work at all?
  on-tb60-lag25     on,  tau_branch 60    **the purchase**
  on-tb60-lag50     on,  tau_branch 60

  off-tb120-lag0    off, tau_branch 120
  on-tb120-lag0     on,  tau_branch 120
  on-tb120-lag50    on,  tau_branch 120   does more dial buy more window?

  on-tag-lag0       on,  bind_tau_pre 300 + tau_act_fast 300
  on-tag-lag25      on,  same
  on-tag-lag150     on,  same             **the intercontinental test**

THE SECOND ROUTE, ADDED BEFORE THE RUN
----------------------------------------
`tau_branch` is not the only way to widen the window; it is only the way that
existed when this note was written. `bind_tau_pre` gives the binding rule its
own presynaptic trace at its own constant, so the window is bought **without
changing what the column computes**. The forward path is provably untouched --
`test_bind_tau_pre_widens_the_window_without_touching_the_forward_path` asserts
the column's output is bit-identical with the parameter declared.

Measured by `lagwindow.py` at `tau_act_fast = bind_tau_pre = 300`: the window is
**154 steps** against a product prediction of 150, and **40% of the binding
update survives a lag of 150** where the default retains essentially none. That
is the first configuration in this project to reach intercontinental lag.

Both routes are in one matrix so they are directly comparable rather than
measured against two different baselines a week apart.

**There is no `off-tag` condition, and that is deliberate.** With `hebbian 0`
both `tau_act_fast` and `bind_tau_pre` are inert -- `act_fast` is only advanced
when binding is on, and the trace is allocated but never read -- so an `off-tag`
condition would be bit-identical to `off-lag0` and could refute nothing. It is
the same shape as sweep 022's `preset-frozen`, caught this time before the run
rather than after. `off-lag0` is the matched reference, and the equality that
makes it so is pinned by a test rather than assumed.

`drain_steps` pinned at 200 everywhere, for the reason sweep 013 and sweep 026
both paid for: a tail that tracks the lag makes the comparison about tail length.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
--------------------------------------
From the measured windows. `tau_branch 120` predicts
`1/(1/50 + 1/120) = 35.3`, and the probe has run consistently 3-8% wide, so
call it 37.

  tau_branch    window    retained at lag 25    retained at lag 50
       15        11.9           0.12                  0.01
       60        28.7           0.42                  0.18
      120        37             0.51                  0.26

Scaling by each condition's own lag-0 gain, and assuming that gain stays near
+0.127:

  on-tb60-lag25    gain +0.04 to +0.07, and **significant at 20 seeds**
  on-tb60-lag50    gain +0.01 to +0.04, probably null
  on-tb120-lag50   gain +0.02 to +0.05, marginal

**The central claim: `on-tb60-lag25` is significantly better than
`off-tb60-lag0`, where the same comparison at `tau_branch 15` was null.** That
is the whole sweep. If the window arithmetic transfers from weight-update
magnitude to decodability, it must.

**And the cost, predicted separately and less confidently:** `off-tb60-lag0`
lands at **0.55 to 0.62** against the 0.620 reference -- a small loss or none.
The reasoning is that a 60-step filter smears a 25-step cue burst, which should
hurt cue *identification*, but the task's difficulty is holding cues across a
100-step gap, and a longer filter helps there. The two pull opposite ways and I
do not know which wins. `off-tb120-lag0` is predicted lower, **0.45 to 0.58**,
because at 120 steps the filter is longer than the gap between cues and should
start merging them.

I am recording that the cost prediction is weak, rather than narrowing the band
to look confident. The window prediction is the one with a measurement behind
it.

**For the tag route**, where the window is 154 steps and the retained fractions
are 0.85 at lag 25 and 0.38 at lag 150:

  on-tag-lag0    gain +0.09 to +0.14, i.e. **at or near the standing +0.127**
  on-tag-lag25   gain +0.08 to +0.13, and significant
  on-tag-lag150  gain +0.04 to +0.09, and **significant** -- the headline

**The tag route's central claim: `on-tag-lag150` beats `off-lag0` at 20 seeds**,
where `on-lag150` was +0.001 at p = 0.88 and `on-lag150-slow` was +0.000 at
p = 1.0000. Two conditions have already failed at this lag, so a win is a real
result and a loss costs the mechanism nothing but a config flag.

**And the cost of the tag route is a precision cost, measured by
`on-tag-lag0 vs on-lag0`.** A wider window scores a broader slice of the
episode's activity, so credit is assigned less precisely even when nothing is
late. Predicted **0.00 to -0.04** -- some loss, not enough to matter. This is
the number I am least confident about and the one most likely to be the
interesting result: if a 154-step window costs nothing at lag 0, then the
narrow window was never buying precision and `tau_act_fast`/`tau_branch` are
simply too small everywhere.

WHAT EACH OUTCOME MEANS
-------------------------
  purchase works,      Latency tolerance is a setting, bought in the forward
  cost is small        path rather than the learning rule. The README says which
                       parameter buys it, what it costs, and that 29 steps is
                       a rack rather than a continent. Best available outcome
                       and still short of intercontinental.

  purchase works,      A real trade with a price tag. Then the question becomes
  cost is large        whether a task that needs distribution is one where the
                       forward-path loss is affordable, which cannot be answered
                       on delayed XOR.

  purchase fails,      The window arithmetic holds for the weight update and not
  cost is small        for the representation -- a bigger update at lag 25 that
                       does not produce a better column. That would mean binding
                       needs *coincidence*, not merely magnitude, and latency
                       tolerance is not buyable from any time constant. Most
                       informative outcome and the one that closes the direction.

  purchase fails,      The dial is simply bad. Nothing learned about latency;
  cost is large        the forward-path change swamped the comparison.

The third is the one to be careful not to explain away, and the one that would
be easiest to misread as "tau_branch 60 is a bad setting" when it is actually a
statement about what binding requires.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TEST
-------------------------
Whether a wider window is a *good idea*. Every condition here asks whether the
gain survives a late modulator; none asks whether the resulting credit
assignment is better than the narrow one at doing its job. `on-tag-lag0 vs
on-lag0` is the closest thing to that question and it is one comparison, on one
task, at one pair of constants.

Nor does it test the obvious refinement: a *cascade* of traces at several
timescales, which would give precision at short lag and tolerance at long lag
instead of trading them. That is the next mechanism if the precision cost here
turns out to be real.

The earlier version of this note said no condition in the sweep reaches
intercontinental lag. That was true of the `tau_branch` route -- its best window
is ~37 steps against 150 -- and it is what motivated building the tag. It is
recorded rather than deleted because it is why the second route exists.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, 500 collection episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, drain_steps
200 everywhere. `tau_branch` 15 / 60 / 120 with `tau_act_fast` 50 for the
forward-path route; `tau_act_fast = bind_tau_pre = 300` with `tau_branch` at its
default 15 for the tag route. `tau_act_fast` is raised for the tag conditions
because the window is a product -- 300 on the presynaptic side alone would still
be capped near 50 by the activity trace.

Collection budget stated because sweep 028 established it is part of the number:
at 500 episodes the standing no-tail gain is +0.074, at 1200 it is +0.060.

THE PURCHASE WORKS, AND THE CENTRAL CLAIM HELD
------------------------------------------------
`on-tb60-lag25` beats its own matched reference by **+0.062 on 20 of 20 seeds at
p = 0.0000**, against a predicted +0.04 to +0.07. At the default `tau_branch`
the same comparison was **null** (+0.016, p = 0.1610). Against the default at the
same lag it is +0.264 on 20/20.

And it survives further than predicted. `on-tb60-lag50` was called "probably
null" and came back **+0.037 at p = 0.0000** -- 45% of the lag-0 gain retained at
a lag of 50, where the default retains nothing measurable. The window arithmetic
transfers from weight-update magnitude to decodability, which was the whole
question.

THE COST PREDICTION WAS WRONG IN DIRECTION, NOT JUST IN SIZE
--------------------------------------------------------------
Predicted: `off-tb60-lag0` lands at **0.55 to 0.62**, a small loss or none,
because a 60-step filter smears a 25-step cue burst.

Measured: **0.838.** Widening the branch filter is worth **+0.218 on 20/20 seeds
at p = 0.0000**, and `tau_branch 120` is worth **+0.298**.

This is the largest single-parameter effect ever measured in this project --
larger than homeostatic settling's +0.197, larger than every learning rule
combined -- and it was found by accident while pricing something else. The note
recorded the cost prediction as the weak one and said the two effects "pull
opposite ways and I do not know which wins". That was the right amount of
confidence and it was still wrong about the sign.

**What it does NOT establish, and the next sweep.** Every condition here runs at
`drain_steps 200`, where the baseline is 0.620 against the 0.802 that the
tail-free configuration measures. So `tau_branch 60/120` may be *compensating
for the drain tail* rather than improving the column generally: 200 silent steps
per episode is a long time for a 15-step filter to forget across, and a 120-step
one forgets less. **Nothing here shows tau_branch helps at drain 0**, and reading
+0.298 as a general improvement would be exactly the baseline-shift error sweep
026 already paid for once. Sweep 034 is `tau_branch` swept at `drain_steps 0`
against the standing 0.802.

THE TAG ROUTE IS REFUTED, INCLUDING THE HEADLINE
--------------------------------------------------
  predicted  on-tag-lag0    +0.09 to +0.14      measured +0.028
  predicted  on-tag-lag25   +0.08 to +0.13      measured +0.038
  predicted  on-tag-lag150  +0.04 to +0.09,     measured +0.019, p = 0.0794
                            **significant**     **NULL -- the central claim**
  predicted  precision cost 0.00 to -0.04       measured **-0.099**

`on-tag-lag150` was the headline of this sweep and it does not clear
significance. And the precision cost is **more than twice the worst case
predicted**: giving binding its own 300-step trace costs 0.099 at lag 0, on 0 of
20 seeds, at p = 0.0000.

The two numbers together say what went wrong, and the note predicted the shape
of it without believing it. A 154-step window scores activity from essentially
the whole episode, so binding strengthens whatever was active at *any* point
rather than what was active *then*. The temporal specificity is what made the
mechanism work, and the tag trades it away faster than it buys tolerance. The
note called this "the number I am least confident about and the one most likely
to be the interesting result" -- it was, and in the opposite direction to the one
named there.

`bind_tau_pre` stays in the library, off by default, because the measurement is
worth keeping and the flag costs nothing. It should not be described as a way to
buy latency tolerance.

WHAT THE TWO ROUTES TOGETHER SAY
----------------------------------
A wider window helps only when the *forward path* is wide enough to match it.
`tau_branch 60` widens what the column integrates and the plasticity window
together, and binding keeps working. `bind_tau_pre 300` widens only the
plasticity window, leaving the rule scoring a 300-step trace against a column
that still computes on 15 steps -- and that mismatch costs more than the
tolerance is worth.

That is a sharper statement than either route alone, and it was only available
because both ran in one matrix.

WHERE THIS LEAVES THE ARCHITECTURE'S CLAIM
--------------------------------------------
Honestly: better than before, and still short.

  inference across columns   150 steps, free (measured, frozen columns)
  learning, default          12-step window, null from lag 25
  learning, tau_branch 60    significant at lag 25 AND lag 50
  learning, anything here    nothing reaches lag 150

`on-tb120-lag50` is null (+0.003, p = 0.7073), but its reference is already at
0.918 and there is almost no headroom left, so that null is uninformative about
the mechanism in the same way sweep 028's 384-neuron condition was.

Intercontinental credit assignment remains unreached. What changed is that
regional latency -- 25 to 50 steps, a rack to a metro area -- is now *measured*
to work rather than hoped for.
