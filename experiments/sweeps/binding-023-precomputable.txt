Sweep 023 -- is the settling operating point precomputable?
===========================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
The previous sweep found that most of what makes this network readable isn't
learning at all -- it's each neuron finding the right sensitivity setting, a
couple of numbers per neuron. Getting there takes about fifty rounds of practice.

The question here: does it need to practise on the *real task* to find those
settings, or would any input with roughly the right statistics do?

If meaningless static works just as well, those fifty rounds are free forever --
you'd compute the settings once and hand them over. If it genuinely needs the
task, then those settings are storing something about the task itself, and the
interesting question becomes what.

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 022 established that the +0.197 from homeostatic settling is an operating
point rather than a learned trajectory: a column handed a settled twin's
threshold and knee, with nothing adapting and `lr=0`, reads 0.752 against 0.582.

What it did not establish is that the operating point is *free*. The twin had
fifty episodes of exposure to the task to find it -- unsupervised, no labels,
`lr=0` throughout, but derived from data.

If the values depend only on the input's marginal statistics, they are
precomputable and fifty episodes of every future run are free. If they depend on
the task's temporal structure, they are not, and the more interesting question
becomes what about timing a per-neuron threshold can encode.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 100 episodes, checkpoints every 10, 96 neurons, lr 0)
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------
The column being measured always runs the real task. Only the *twin* that finds
the operating point sees different input.

  all               no preset, everything adapting          (the 022 baseline)
  preset-task       twin on the real task                   (= 022's preset-frozen)
  preset-shuffled   twin on the task's marginals with timing destroyed
  preset-noise      twin on Bernoulli events at the task's overall rate

`shuffled` permutes each channel's time series independently, so per-channel
event count and amplitude distribution are preserved *exactly* while cue timing,
the cue-to-go relationship and cross-channel synchrony are gone. Exactness is
the point: a matched-rate Bernoulli stream alone would confound "homeostasis
does not need timing" with "the rate was not matched closely enough". `noise` is
kept as the weaker version, to show how far down the ladder the answer holds.

A MEASUREMENT BUG THIS SWEEP FOUND, AND WHAT IT COSTS SWEEP 022
----------------------------------------------------------------
The sparsity column reported `column.sparsity`, which returns the firing-rate
EMA. That EMA only advances while `learning` is True, so **at checkpoint 0,
before any training episode has run, it returns `target_rate` unchanged from
initialisation.** Every condition read 0.0300 there. It looked like a
measurement, and it was the initial value of a variable.

Sparsity is now measured during the probe itself, by counting emissions. The
first thing that showed:

  all, episode 0     sparsity 0.0022, not 0.0300

**The untrained column is nearly silent** -- a fifteenth of target rate. So
sweep 022's episode-0 row concealed the largest fact in it: the 0.582 baseline
every mechanism in this project is measured against is a column that is barely
firing.

That does not overturn sweep 022's conclusion, it sharpens it. "The +0.197 is an
operating point" becomes something more specific and more testable: **the
operating point is largely a firing rate, and homeostasis's first job is to
bring a silent column up to target.** Presetting theta and knee supplies that
immediately.

What it does invalidate is any reading of sweep 022's episode-0 sparsity row.
Recorded in AUDIT.md. The rows from episode 10 onward were measured after
training episodes, where the EMA does advance, so they stand.

PREDICTION (recorded before the 20-seed run)
----------------------------------------------
One seed is in hand, as it was for sweeps 020 and 022, and the prediction is
informed by it. Stating that rather than pretending otherwise.

  all               0.582 at episode 0 rising to ~0.76; sparsity 0.002 -> 0.031
  preset-task       0.74 to 0.77, flat            sparsity ~0.020
  preset-shuffled   0.70 to 0.74, flat            sparsity 0.026 to 0.031
  preset-noise      0.62 to 0.68, flat            sparsity 0.010 to 0.018

**The central claim: `preset-shuffled` lands below `preset-task` by 0.02 to
0.06, while running at a firing rate at least as close to target.** That is the
combination that decides it, and the one seed already shows it: shuffled sits at
sparsity 0.0288 against task's 0.0204 -- *better* calibrated -- and decodes
0.722 against 0.833. If it held only at worse sparsity the result would be about
activity level and not about the operating point.

So: **if shuffled is below task at equal-or-better sparsity, the operating point
is not precomputable from marginal input statistics.** It encodes something
about the task's temporal structure, and the "fifty free episodes" idea is dead.

WHAT WOULD REFUTE IT
---------------------
`preset-shuffled` within 0.02 of `preset-task`. Then the operating point depends
only on marginals, the twin can be settled on synthetic input, and every run
starts fifty episodes ahead. That is the outcome I would prefer and the one I
am predicting against.

A CONFOUND NAMED IN ADVANCE
----------------------------
`preset-noise` is expected to run under-active (one seed: 0.0125 against a 0.03
target). If it decodes worst, that is **not** clean evidence about operating
points, because a column at 40% of target rate decodes badly for reasons that
have nothing to do with what its threshold encodes. It is reported as a ladder
rung, not as a third data point, and the argument rests on `shuffled`, where the
sparsity confound runs the *wrong way* for the conclusion.

WHAT THIS SWEEP CANNOT SAY
---------------------------
Nothing here identifies *what* about timing the threshold encodes. A refuted
"precomputable" leaves that wide open, and the honest next step would be to ask
which of theta and knee carries it -- preset one from the task and the other
from shuffled input. Not folded in here.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 100 episodes, checkpoints every 10, 300 probe episodes, 96 neurons,
column lr 0, binding off, lateral off, `--preset-from 50`.

THE PREDICTION IS REFUTED, IN THE DIRECTION I SAID I WOULD PREFER
------------------------------------------------------------------
The central claim was that `preset-shuffled` lands 0.02 to 0.06 *below*
`preset-task`. It lands **+0.051 above** it -- 0.803 against 0.752 -- and above
`all`'s 0.755 as well, while running at a firing rate nearer target (0.0273
against 0.0217).

So on this evidence the operating point is not merely precomputable from input
that has no task structure: **it is better computed that way.** A twin settled on
timing-destroyed input hands over a threshold and knee that beat both the twin
settled on the task and a hundred episodes of ordinary settling.

The refuting condition named in advance was "shuffled within 0.02 of task". The
actual outcome is outside that band on the other side, which the prediction did
not enumerate and should have.

WHY ONE SEED POINTED THE OTHER WAY, AND WHAT THAT COSTS
--------------------------------------------------------
The prediction said one seed was in hand and that it informed the numbers. At
seed 0: task 0.833, shuffled 0.722 -- a gap of -0.111, pointing hard the wrong
way. At 20 seeds the same comparison is +0.051.

That is rule 3's calibration reproducing itself exactly: an effect of +0.059 at
three seeds went to +0.023 at eight and to zero at twenty, twice. Here a
-0.111 at one seed went to +0.051 at twenty -- it did not shrink, it reversed.

Stating it plainly because the practice of running one seed before writing a
prediction is defensible for *designing* conditions and dangerous for *setting*
expectations, and this is the second time in this project a single seed has
produced a confident prediction that was wrong.

THE PAIRED TESTS, FROM THE RE-RUN
----------------------------------
The first run printed the report table and stopped, so the numbers above were a
comparison of means with no p-value -- which rule 3 does not accept and which is
what sweeps 001-010 were repeatedly wrong to call a result. The cause was
structural: `trajectory.py` recorded its curve as a dict keyed by checkpoint and
`paired_test.py` reads a flat metric per row. Rows now carry `final` and
`final_sparsity`; the workflow tests both.

  preset-shuffled vs preset-task   final    +0.051  14/20  p = 0.0032
                                   sparsity +0.006  20/20  p = 0.0000
  preset-shuffled vs all           final    +0.048  15/20  p = 0.0049
                                   sparsity -0.002   0/20  p = 0.0000
  preset-task     vs all           final    -0.003  10/20  p = 0.9108
                                   sparsity -0.008   0/20  p = 0.0000
  preset-noise    vs preset-task   final    -0.061   3/20  p = 0.0033
                                   sparsity -0.005   2/20  p = 0.0003

**The headline survives testing.** An operating point found on timing-destroyed
input beats one found on the real task (p = 0.0032) and beats a hundred episodes
of ordinary settling (p = 0.0049).

`preset-task vs all` is null at p = 0.91, which is the cleanest statement of
sweep 022's finding: presetting reproduces a hundred episodes of settling
exactly, and adds nothing on its own. The whole gain over `all` comes from *what
the twin was settled on*.

AND THE SPARSITY COLUMN KILLS MY OWN PROPOSED MECHANISM
--------------------------------------------------------
The mechanism suggested above was that a rate controller settling on a bursty
stream calibrates for a mixture of drive regimes and sits at neither, while a
stationary stream gives it one regime to converge on. That predicts the
best-calibrated condition decodes best.

Line the two orderings up:

  firing rate    all 0.030  >  shuffled 0.027  >  task 0.022  >  noise 0.017
  decodability   shuffled 0.803  >  all 0.755  ~  task 0.752  >  noise 0.691

**They do not match.** `all` sits exactly on the 0.03 target -- better
calibrated than `shuffled` -- and decodes 0.048 worse. So whatever the shuffled
twin is handing over, it is not a firing rate closer to target, and the
explanation offered above is wrong.

That is worth more than the mechanism would have been. It rules out the
confound this sweep was built to guard against, in the strongest possible form:
the condition that wins is *not* the one with the best activity level, so the
result cannot be read as an artefact of activity. What theta and knee carry that
survives the destruction of timing, and beats the values found with timing
intact, is open and unexplained.

THE LADDER RUNG BEHAVED AS PREDICTED
--------------------------------------
`preset-noise` reaches 0.691 at sparsity 0.0170, against a 0.03 target -- 57% of
target rate, under-active exactly as the prediction said it would be. It is the
lowest of the three and it is *not* clean evidence, for the reason recorded in
advance. What it does show is that the gap between it and `shuffled` is large
(0.112), and the only difference between them is that `shuffled` preserves
per-channel marginals exactly while `noise` is uniform across channels. So
per-channel structure matters even though timing appears not to.

A MECHANISM WORTH TESTING, NOT ASSERTING
------------------------------------------
Shuffled input is temporally uniform; the task is bursty, with cue and go
periods against a sparse background. A rate controller settling on a bursty
stream converges to a threshold calibrated for a *mixture* of drive regimes and
sits at neither; on a stationary stream it converges to one. The sparsity column
is consistent with that -- shuffled ends at 0.0273 against task's 0.0217, closer
to the 0.03 target.

That is a hypothesis. The test is direct: measure per-neuron |theta| difference
between the two twins and check whether the task-settled thresholds are
systematically higher, which burst adaptation would predict. Not run.
