Sweep 012 -- engram allocation
==============================

QUESTION
--------
Eleven sweeps have failed to show the three-factor rule beating a frozen
column. Sweep 009 stated the gap sharpest: a frozen column gives linear 0.639
against MLP 0.891, and closing a +0.25 gap like that is exactly a learning
rule's job. So the question here is not "can the modulator be tuned" -- it is
whether a *different kind* of rule, one that does not depend on the error
signal being right, writes structure the linear decoder can use.

The candidate comes from the memory-allocation literature (Han et al. 2007;
Yiu et al. 2014; Cai et al. 2016; Josselyn & Tonegawa 2020). Biology does not
smear a memory over whoever was active. Excitability drifts apart beforehand,
the most excitable ~15% win a competition to be recruited, winning costs
excitability so the next memory lands elsewhere, and Hebbian plasticity binds
the winners into an assembly.

Our homeostasis was doing the opposite of step one: driving every neuron to an
identical firing rate, which destroys the excitability differences allocation
needs. That is why this is not an additive change.

PREDICTION (recorded before the 20-seed run)
--------------------------------------------
Moderate confidence the mechanism improves linear decodability over a frozen
column, because the binding term writes input-correlated structure into W
without routing through the modulator. Low confidence it survives 20 seeds at
the magnitude seen at 1-2 seeds (+0.10), because two previous "breakthroughs"
in this project decayed to zero between 3 seeds and 20.

Specific prediction: alloc+hebb > off on `linear`, p < 0.05, effect between
+0.02 and +0.06. And `alloc` alone (no binding) shows no gain, because
recruitment by itself changes no weights.

WHAT WAS BUILT WRONG FIRST (three attempts)
-------------------------------------------
Recorded because each failure was invisible end-to-end and only a direct
measurement of the mechanism's parts found it.

1. Recruitment on `act_fast > margin * act_slow`. Self-normalising: a neuron
   made more excitable raises its own baseline along with its activity, so the
   criterion cancels exactly the variable meant to drive it. Excitability
   correlated with recruitment at -0.40 -- backwards. Consecutive engrams
   overlapped at 3.8x chance, i.e. no allocation was happening at all.

2. Scoring against statistics accumulated every timestep. That compares a
   neuron's activity at recruitment time against the distribution of all
   timesteps, most of which are nothing like it, so units that reliably answer
   the go cue look extraordinary at every allocation. The criterion measured
   which neurons are phasic, not what the episode engaged. Identical in form to
   the readout bug where an episode-level decision vector was standardised with
   per-timestep variance. Fixing it: rotate 3.83 -> 1.87, separate 0.011 ->
   0.021.

3. Excitability routed through the firing threshold. Measured
   corr(excitability, activity z-score) = -0.41, structural and identical at
   every setting: driving a neuron harder depletes its short-term synaptic
   resources, and the emitted value is scaled by what remains, so a more
   excitable neuron reports *less* activity per event. The two ingredients of
   allocation were cancelling. Decoupling them (excite_threshold=0,
   excite_gain=2) flips recruitment from anti-predicting excitability (-0.22)
   to predicting it (+0.20), and is the only setting measured where the
   mechanism does what it is described as doing.

   Worth stating plainly: biology has no reason to separate those two paths.
   We do, and the reason is a property of our emission model, not of neurons.

MEASURED ALONG THE WAY
----------------------
99.7% of the column's state vector at answer time is an episode-independent
common mode. The part that identifies which cues occurred is a small residual
on top of it -- pairwise correlation +0.23 within a cue pattern, -0.08 across.
Any criterion scaled by a neuron's magnitude rather than its variability is
blind to that residual. This is the fourth appearance of the same bug family in
this project (readout mean/variance, elig_rms, recruitment baseline, statistics
timescale) and is now written into CLAUDE.md as a standing check.

PRELIMINARY (1-2 seeds -- NOT a result)
---------------------------------------
condition      rotate  separate   linear    MLP
off              --       --       0.837   0.990
alloc           0.66    +0.039     0.757   0.987
alloc+hebb      0.89    +0.060     0.933   1.000
no-refract      1.60    +0.076     0.927   0.993

rotate below 1.0 is genuine rotation -- successive engrams overlapping *less*
than independent sampling would give, which is the allocation refractory
working as designed for the first time. no-refract at 1.60 confirms the drop
is what causes it.

The tension the two metrics were built to expose is real and visible:
removing the refractory raises separation (+0.060 -> +0.076) while destroying
rotation (0.89 -> 1.60). Neither number alone would have shown that.

Note also that the frozen baseline here reads 0.837 linear, well above the
0.639 recorded in sweep 009. Different harness settings (300 episodes, 500
collection episodes, 96 neurons), so these are not comparable across sweeps --
only within this table, where every condition shares a seed and a harness.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds
-------------------------
condition      excite   alloc  rotate  separate   linear     MLP  sparsity
off             0.000   0.000   0.000     0.000    0.802   0.987     0.031
alloc          +0.043   0.163   0.642    +0.036    0.758   0.978     0.033
alloc+hebb     -0.012   0.176   0.815    +0.052    0.831   0.999     0.037
no-refract     +0.016   0.174   1.661    +0.061    0.844   0.998     0.036

Paired permutation on `linear`:
  alloc+hebb vs off          +0.029, 14/20 seeds, p = 0.0176  SIGNIFICANT
  alloc     vs off           -0.044,  5/20 seeds, p = 0.0099  SIGNIFICANT, negative
  alloc+hebb vs no-refract   -0.014,  8/20 seeds, p = 0.15    no difference

The prediction held, and landed inside the band it named: +0.029 against a
predicted +0.02 to +0.06, p < 0.05. The 1-seed reading of +0.10 did not
survive, exactly as predicted -- and the prediction that it would not is the
reason this file is worth anything.

This is the first mechanism in the project to beat a frozen column at twenty
seeds. Worth stating precisely what that does and does not mean.

WHAT IT MEANS
-------------
It is a *local* gain. The RLS readout (sweep 010) produced +0.032 at p = 0.0098
and required a globally pooled correlation matrix, which sweep 011 showed does
not decompose; the default was reverted. This is +0.029 at p = 0.0176 with
nothing pooled anywhere -- every operation is a neuron reading its own state.
Comparable size, bought without breaking the rule the architecture exists to
serve. That comparison is the point, not the raw number.

It is also an unsupervised gain. Recruitment reads only the *presence* of a
modulator release, never its sign or its target, so nothing here depends on the
error signal being correct. That matters because the supervised path is the one
that has failed eleven times.

WHAT IT DOES NOT MEAN
---------------------
The allocation machinery is not what earned it. Three lines say so:

  - `alloc` alone is reliably WORSE than off (-0.044, p = 0.0099). Recruitment
    and refractoriness with no weight change actively damage the
    representation.
  - `no-refract` reads 0.844 against alloc+hebb's 0.831, i.e. removing the
    allocation refractory is if anything better, and certainly not worse
    (p = 0.15).
  - `excite` is -0.012 in the full condition. The excitability bias that all
    three redesigns were aimed at is doing nothing measurable here.

So the honest reading is that **Hebbian binding gated by a salience signal is
carrying the entire effect**, and the allocation scaffolding around it is at
best neutral and at worst harmful. The refractory does buy the one thing it was
built for -- rotate 0.815 against no-refract's 1.661, engrams overlapping less
than independent sampling -- but that rotation is not yet worth anything
downstream on this task, which asks nothing of memory separation. A capacity or
continual-learning benchmark is where it would have to pay off, and this task
is not one.

Also unconfirmed: this is linear decodability of the representation, not
end-to-end accuracy. Sweep 009 is the standing warning that the two can come
apart. The next run should be e2e.

NEXT
----
1. e2e confirmation of alloc+hebb at 20 seeds.
2. Strip the mechanism to Hebbian binding alone, no excitability, no
   recruitment competition -- just salience-gated Hebbian LTP with synaptic
   scaling for competition. If that matches 0.831, the entire engram apparatus
   is scaffolding and should be deleted rather than defended.
3. Only if (2) shows allocation contributing: a benchmark that asks for
   memory separation, where rotation could be worth its cost.
