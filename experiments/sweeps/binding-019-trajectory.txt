Sweep 019 -- when during training does the binding gain arrive?
===============================================================

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 018 left an oddity. Binding needs roughly its full 300 episodes: cutting
it to 150 to fund a static period for the readout gives back nearly all the
gain (phase 0.731 against catchup 0.770, both with 150 idle episodes). That is
strange, because the baseline binding scores against converges early, so late
binding events should contribute progressively less.

Something is accumulating late that no diagnostic measures. This measures it --
linear decodability of the column state at every 25 episodes, rather than only
at the end.

The curve separates two explanations 018 could not:

  BINDING IS SLOW. Decodability keeps climbing past episode 150. Then `phase`
  failed because 150 episodes of binding genuinely is not enough, and the
  question becomes why a rule whose baseline settles early keeps improving.

  THE READOUT IS SLOW. Decodability is already near 0.876 by episode 150. Then
  the representation was ready and `phase` should have worked, so the 150 idle
  episodes were not enough for the readout to converge -- which contradicts the
  delta rule converging in well under that, and would need explaining.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
------------------------------------
Binding is slow, at about 70/30.

  episode 0     both conditions 0.80 +/- 0.01, and they MUST agree -- episode 0
                is the frozen column in both, so a gap there means the probe is
                measuring something other than binding and the run is void.
  episode 150   on at 0.835 to 0.855, i.e. roughly half to two thirds of the
                final +0.074
  episode 300   on at 0.876, off flat at 0.802 throughout

The reasoning is arithmetic rather than intuition: `phase` (150 bind, 150 idle)
delivered +0.023 end to end where `catchup` (300 bind, 150 idle) delivered
+0.063. If end-to-end gain is roughly proportional to the representation gain
available when binding stopped, then decodability at 150 should sit around
0.802 + 0.074 * (0.023/0.063) = 0.829. Predicting slightly above that because
the transfer is unlikely to be perfectly linear.

WHAT WOULD SURPRISE ME
-----------------------
A curve that is still rising steeply at 300. That would mean the mechanism has
not converged at all within the budget every sweep so far has used, and every
number recorded for it -- including the headline +0.074 -- is a snapshot of an
unfinished process rather than a property of the mechanism. It would also make
the 018 result unsurprising in hindsight and mean the interesting sweep is a
longer one.

Recording that in advance because it is the reading most likely to be
rationalised away as "it works, it just needs longer".

A FIX THIS SWEEP FORCED
------------------------
Writing the probe required asserting that probing does not perturb training,
and the first version of that assertion failed. Three accumulators advanced
during evaluation: `_steps`, which schedules synaptic scaling, and the `rate`
and `engagement` EMAs, which drive the two homeostatic loops. All are read only
by learning operations, so a purely observational pass was shifting the
column's homeostatic trajectory -- thresholds by 1.5e-2, knees by 4.5e-2, W by
8.0e-4 over twelve episodes.

Fixed in the column rather than worked around in the probe. No recorded result
is affected: every experiment before this one evaluated *after* training
finished, so the leak had nothing to leak into. It surfaced only because this
is the first measurement taken mid-training.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, checkpoints every 25, 300 probe episodes per
checkpoint, 96 neurons, column lr 0. Same configuration as sweeps 014-018 so
the endpoint is directly comparable.

THE MAIN HYPOTHESIS IS REFUTED
-------------------------------
"Binding is slow" was called at 70/30. It is wrong. The gap reaches +0.097 by
episode 150 and then goes flat, oscillating between +0.079 and +0.102 for the
remaining half of training with no trend. **The representation gain is complete
by episode 150.**

The one number predicted precisely was right: `on` at episode 150 was called at
0.835-0.855 and came in at 0.841. Being right about the level while wrong about
the shape is worth noticing -- the arithmetic that produced 0.841 assumed the
end-to-end gain scales with representation gain, and it does not.

SO THE READOUT IS THE SLOW PART
--------------------------------
Line the three results up:

  representation gain complete at episode 150          (this sweep)
  phase   150 bind + 150 idle -> +0.023 end to end     (018)
  catchup 300 bind + 150 idle -> +0.063 end to end     (017)

At episode 150 the representation is as good as it will ever get. `phase`
stopped binding exactly then, gave the readout 150 idle episodes, and still
delivered a third of what `catchup` delivered. The difference is not what the
column had learned -- that was identical -- it is how long the readout had to
work against a settled column: 150 episodes for `phase`, roughly 300 for
`catchup`, whose representation also stopped moving at 150 but which kept
training for another 150 before its idle period began.

**The delta readout needs roughly 300 episodes against a settled representation
to exploit it.** That is a statement about the readout's sample efficiency, not
about the mechanism's speed, and it means sweep 018's framing -- "binding needs
its full 300 episodes" -- was the wrong reading of a correct measurement.

TWO OTHER PREDICTIONS, BOTH WRONG
----------------------------------
Episode 0 was predicted at 0.80 in both conditions. It is 0.582. The 0.802
figure from sweeps 014-018 is the frozen column *after 300 episodes*, and a
frozen column is not an untrained one: `lr=0` disables the three-factor rule
but homeostasis and synaptic scaling keep running. Confusing "frozen" with
"untrained" is a mistake worth naming, because it is easy to make and the two
words get used interchangeably in this repo.

Which surfaces something nobody measured: **the `off` condition climbs from
0.582 to 0.779 in the first fifty episodes, on unsupervised homeostatic
settling alone.** That is +0.197 of decodability from threshold and knee
adaptation with no learning rule of any kind -- more than twice what binding
adds. It then plateaus and wanders in 0.744-0.779 for the rest of training.

The prediction also said `off` would be flat throughout. It is flat only after
episode 50.

Absolute values here sit below sweeps 014-018 (on 0.852 against 0.876, off
0.772 against 0.802) because this probe uses 300 probe episodes against
binding.py's 500, with a different split. The *gap* is +0.081 against +0.074,
which agrees. Compare gaps across sweeps, not levels.

WHAT THIS CHANGES
-----------------
Sweep 018 concluded "binding needs roughly its full 300 episodes and the
readout needs a static period after, and the two cannot come out of one
budget". The first half of that is now wrong. Binding is done at 150. What
cannot come out of one budget is the *readout's* 300 episodes against a settled
column.

The fix therefore is not a binding schedule -- 018 already showed every
schedule inside the budget is null, and now it is clear why: rescheduling
binding cannot help when binding was never the bottleneck. The fix is a readout
that converges faster. RLS is exactly that and delivers +0.043 with no schedule
change at all (017), but needs a globally pooled correlation matrix that sweep
011 showed does not decompose.

So the next thing to build is local decorrelation inside the column -- lateral
inhibition -- which would let a cheap local readout extract what RLS extracts
by pooling. That has been on the list since sweep 011 and this is the second
independent result pointing at it.
