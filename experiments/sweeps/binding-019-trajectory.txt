Sweep 019 -- when during training does the binding gain arrive?
===============================================================

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 018 left an oddity. Binding needs roughly its full 300 episodes: cutting
it to 150 to fund a static period for the readout gives back nearly all the
gain (phase 0.731 against catchup 0.770, both with 150 idle episodes). That is
strange, because the baseline binding scores against converges early, so late
binding events should contribute progressively less.

Something is accumulating late that no diagnostic measures. This measures it --
linear decodability of the column state at every 25 episodes, rather than only
at the end.

The curve separates two explanations 018 could not:

  BINDING IS SLOW. Decodability keeps climbing past episode 150. Then `phase`
  failed because 150 episodes of binding genuinely is not enough, and the
  question becomes why a rule whose baseline settles early keeps improving.

  THE READOUT IS SLOW. Decodability is already near 0.876 by episode 150. Then
  the representation was ready and `phase` should have worked, so the 150 idle
  episodes were not enough for the readout to converge -- which contradicts the
  delta rule converging in well under that, and would need explaining.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
------------------------------------
Binding is slow, at about 70/30.

  episode 0     both conditions 0.80 +/- 0.01, and they MUST agree -- episode 0
                is the frozen column in both, so a gap there means the probe is
                measuring something other than binding and the run is void.
  episode 150   on at 0.835 to 0.855, i.e. roughly half to two thirds of the
                final +0.074
  episode 300   on at 0.876, off flat at 0.802 throughout

The reasoning is arithmetic rather than intuition: `phase` (150 bind, 150 idle)
delivered +0.023 end to end where `catchup` (300 bind, 150 idle) delivered
+0.063. If end-to-end gain is roughly proportional to the representation gain
available when binding stopped, then decodability at 150 should sit around
0.802 + 0.074 * (0.023/0.063) = 0.829. Predicting slightly above that because
the transfer is unlikely to be perfectly linear.

WHAT WOULD SURPRISE ME
-----------------------
A curve that is still rising steeply at 300. That would mean the mechanism has
not converged at all within the budget every sweep so far has used, and every
number recorded for it -- including the headline +0.074 -- is a snapshot of an
unfinished process rather than a property of the mechanism. It would also make
the 018 result unsurprising in hindsight and mean the interesting sweep is a
longer one.

Recording that in advance because it is the reading most likely to be
rationalised away as "it works, it just needs longer".

A FIX THIS SWEEP FORCED
------------------------
Writing the probe required asserting that probing does not perturb training,
and the first version of that assertion failed. Three accumulators advanced
during evaluation: `_steps`, which schedules synaptic scaling, and the `rate`
and `engagement` EMAs, which drive the two homeostatic loops. All are read only
by learning operations, so a purely observational pass was shifting the
column's homeostatic trajectory -- thresholds by 1.5e-2, knees by 4.5e-2, W by
8.0e-4 over twelve episodes.

Fixed in the column rather than worked around in the probe. No recorded result
is affected: every experiment before this one evaluated *after* training
finished, so the leak had nothing to leak into. It surfaced only because this
is the first measurement taken mid-training.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, checkpoints every 25, 300 probe episodes per
checkpoint, 96 neurons, column lr 0. Same configuration as sweeps 014-018 so
the endpoint is directly comparable.
