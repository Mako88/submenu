Sweep 037 -- what is the dendritic plateau actually worth?
==========================================================

IN PLAIN TERMS
--------------
The single biggest structural idea in this model is that a neuron is not a
summing device. Each of its dendritic branches does its own little computation
first -- it stays quiet until enough of its inputs arrive together, then jumps.
That is what is supposed to make one neuron here worth more than one row of a
matrix multiply, and it is the reason the model is built out of branches at all.

Nobody has ever measured what it buys. There is a test that switching it off
changes the output, which only shows it is plugged in. There is a long note
about a bug it once had. There is no number.

So: turn it off and see. If the network barely notices, the central structural
claim of the design is decorative, and a great deal of complexity is being
carried for nothing. If it matters a lot, that is the strongest argument the
architecture has and it should be stated with a figure.

QUESTION
--------
`DESIGN.md` section 4 is titled "Scale-free dendritic plateau" and is entirely
about the *bug* it once had -- an absolute knee sitting at 1.0 while branch
potentials settled around 0.067, so the nonlinearity never engaged and every
neuron "silently degenerated into a linear summer". The fix and its guard are
documented. The benefit is not.

`test_plateau_nonlinearity_reaches_the_output` compares `plateau 0.0` against
`1.2` and asserts they differ. That is a connection test -- it establishes the
mechanism is wired in, which is exactly what the bug had broken -- and says
nothing about magnitude.

Sweep 025 ran a `no-knee` condition and is sometimes read as covering this. It
is not: it disabled knee *adaptation*, leaving the plateau in place at a fixed
knee, and found -0.039 (p = 0.0130). That measures the homeostasis, not the
nonlinearity.

Meanwhile `DESIGN.md`'s opening states the case in the strongest terms: a
biological neuron "is not a summer", each branch "applies a local NMDA-plateau
nonlinearity, making one neuron closer to a two-layer network that detects
*coincident, clustered* input", and this is kept "because it buys computation
per message, and messages are the expensive resource."

That is a claim about how much computation per message. It has a number and
nobody has measured it.

CONDITIONS  (20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding off, drain 0)
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  plateau-1.2   the default                    reproduces the standing 0.802
  plateau-0     phi is linear; every branch becomes a plain weighted sum
  plateau-0.6   half strength
  plateau-2.4   double strength

`plateau-0` is the condition the sweep exists for. The other two ask a second
question worth asking once the flag is exposed: whether 1.2 was ever tuned, or
is simply the value that was written down first.

Binding off throughout, `drain 0`, so `plateau-1.2` must reproduce **0.802** or
the sweep is not comparable to anything.

**A confound named in advance.** Turning the plateau off changes the branch
potentials' scale, so threshold and knee homeostasis will re-settle to a
different operating point -- and sweeps 022/023 established that the operating
point carries most of a frozen column's decodability. So a drop at `plateau-0`
could be the missing nonlinearity *or* the column landing somewhere worse.
`binding.py` records `sparsity` per row, and if the conditions differ in firing
rate then the comparison is partly an operating-point comparison and the note
must say so rather than reporting the difference as the plateau's value. That
is exactly the trap sweep 023 caught in the rate-calibration hypothesis, and it
is why sparsity is read alongside every number here.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
--------------------------------------
  plateau-1.2   0.802   (established; anything else voids the sweep)
  plateau-0     0.68 to 0.76
  plateau-0.6   0.77 to 0.82
  plateau-2.4   0.76 to 0.82

**The central claim: turning the plateau off costs between 0.04 and 0.12 --
real, clearly significant at 20 seeds, and far less than "one neuron is a
two-layer network" implies.**

The reasoning is that the plateau is not the only nonlinearity in the path. The
soma has a threshold with a soft crossing, short-term plasticity is strongly
nonlinear in its own right and is what carries the memory (0.527 -> 0.864), and
the readout is fitted on top. Removing one nonlinearity from a stack of them
should hurt without being catastrophic.

**And a second prediction, weaker: 1.2 is not optimal, and neither 0.6 nor 2.4
differs from it by more than 0.03.** If a value nobody tuned turns out to be a
sharp optimum, that is worth knowing and slightly suspicious.

-------------------------
  plateau-0 costs a      The structural claim is right and now has a figure.
  lot (>0.15)            DESIGN.md's "computation per message" argument gets the
                         number it has been asserting without, and branch count
                         becomes a parameter worth sweeping.

  plateau-0 costs        Predicted. The plateau earns its place and the
  0.04 to 0.12           two-layer-network framing is overstated. DESIGN.md's
                         opening gets the measured margin instead of the
                         rhetoric, the same correction the routed modulator
                         section just took.

  plateau-0 costs        The model's main departure from a summing unit is
  nothing                decorative on this task. That would not mean deleting
                         it -- delayed XOR may simply not ask for coincidence
                         detection -- but it would mean the claim cannot be made
                         from anything measured here, and the honest move is a
                         task that does ask.

  sparsity differs       Then part of whatever is measured is the operating
  across conditions      point, not the plateau, and the number is an upper
                         bound on the plateau's value rather than an estimate of
                         it. Say so; do not report the difference as the
                         mechanism's worth.

WHAT THIS DOES NOT TEST
-------------------------
Branch *count*. The plateau's value presumably depends on there being 8 branches
of 16 synapses rather than 1 of 128, and that decomposition is a separate sweep
which nothing has run either.

Nor does it test coincidence detection directly. Delayed XOR asks for memory
across a gap, not for detecting clustered simultaneous input, so this sweep
measures the plateau's contribution *to this task* and a null here would be
weak evidence about the mechanism in general.

SETTINGS
--------
20 seeds, 300 episodes, 500 collection episodes, 96 neurons, lr 0, binding and
lateral off, `drain_steps 0`, everything else at defaults. Sparsity read
alongside decodability on every condition.
