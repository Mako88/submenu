Sweep 017 -- why does the representation gain not reach the readout?
====================================================================

QUESTION
--------
Sweep 016 measured the mechanism end to end and got nothing: +0.007 on 11/20
seeds, p = 0.68, against an offline representation gain of +0.074. The null
stands. This asks why, because the two available explanations imply completely
different next steps and picking one by intuition would be guessing.

  1. EXTRACTION. The information is there and the delta readout cannot pull it
     out. Sweep 010 established that RLS closes the online/offline gap on this
     model (+0.032, p = 0.0098), so if the binding gain shows up under RLS and
     not under delta, the mechanism works and the shipped readout is the
     bottleneck.

  2. NON-STATIONARITY. The offline decoder is fitted to the column's *final*
     state. The online readout learns while binding is still changing that
     state underneath it, spending its budget chasing a moving target. Tested
     by freezing binding after training and giving the readout 150 more
     episodes against a now-static column -- which is exactly the state the
     offline probe fits.

Sweep 016's recorded response named (1). (2) is added because the evidence
already points at it: the `on` condition had higher seed variance than `off`
(0.069 vs 0.054) at a nearly identical mean, which is what a readout fighting a
moving representation looks like, not what an inert mechanism looks like.

CONDITIONS
----------
  rls-off / rls-on          --readout-rule rls
  catchup-off / catchup-on  --catchup 150 (binding frozen, readout continues)

Both paired against their own control, so each answers its own question without
depending on the other. Same 20 seeds, 300 episodes, 96 neurons, column lr 0,
drain_steps 0 as in 016.

PREDICTION (recorded before the run)
------------------------------------
Non-stationarity is the better bet, at maybe 60/40.

  catchup: +0.03 to +0.06, p < 0.05. If binding leaves the representation
  better and the readout simply has not caught up, 150 static episodes should
  recover most of the offline gap. A delta readout converges on this task in
  well under that.

  rls: +0.01 to +0.04, and possibly not significant. RLS raises both
  conditions, and if extraction were the whole story sweep 016 would have shown
  *some* gain rather than 9% of the expected one.

  Both null is the outcome that would hurt most, and it is not unlikely. It
  would mean the offline +0.074 reflects something a fresh decoder can exploit
  that no online learner reaches from either direction -- at which point the
  honest reading is that binding improves a *measurement* of the column and not
  the model, and the mechanism should be reported that way or dropped.

Naming that last branch explicitly, because after a null and two follow-ups the
temptation is to keep adding conditions until one is significant. If both come
back null, the answer is that the mechanism does not pay end to end on this
task, and the next move is a different task, not a third readout.

RESULT -- 20 paired seeds
-------------------------
condition      eval accuracy      paired difference
e2e-off        0.709 +/- 0.054
e2e-on         0.715 +/- 0.069    +0.007, 11/20, p = 0.6834   (sweep 016)
rls-off        0.759 +/- 0.053
rls-on         0.803 +/- 0.058    +0.043, 15/20, p = 0.0063   SIGNIFICANT
catchup-off    0.707 +/- 0.060
catchup-on     0.770 +/- 0.070    +0.063, 17/20, p = 0.0013   SIGNIFICANT

Both follow-ups are positive and significant. The mechanism does pay end to
end; sweep 016's null was about the readout, not about binding.

The prediction was close. It called non-stationarity ahead of extraction at
60/40 -- correct, catchup (+0.063) beats rls (+0.043). catchup was predicted at
+0.03 to +0.06 and came in at +0.063, just above the range. rls was predicted
at +0.01 to +0.04 and "possibly not significant"; it came in at +0.043,
p = 0.0063, so extraction matters more than expected.

THE CONTROL NOBODY DESIGNED
----------------------------
catchup-off reads 0.707 against e2e-off's 0.709. Identical, within noise.

That is the whole argument in one line. The catchup condition adds 150 extra
readout-only episodes, and when there is no binding those episodes do
*nothing* -- the readout had already converged. They are worth +0.063 only
when binding has been changing the representation underneath it. The readout
does not need more time in general; it needs time against a column that has
stopped moving.

This fell out of running the control for its own sake rather than because
anyone predicted it would be informative, which is an argument for always
running the control.

WHAT THIS MEANS
---------------
Sweep 016's null was real and is now explained. Binding improves the
representation, and the online delta readout cannot follow it *while it is
still changing*. Two independent routes recover most of the gain:

  - Give the readout more capacity (RLS): +0.043. Sweep 011 showed RLS needs a
    globally pooled correlation matrix that does not decompose, so this route
    costs the locality rule.
  - Give the readout a static target (catchup): +0.063. Costs nothing
    architecturally -- it is a training schedule, entirely local, and it is the
    larger of the two.

The second is the interesting one, and it points somewhere specific: binding
and readout adaptation are competing on the same timescale. Biology separates
them -- consolidation runs on a slower clock than the synaptic changes it
consolidates -- and the fix here is likely the same shape. A binding rate that
decays over training, or a two-phase schedule, should recover the gain without
a separate catch-up period and without touching the readout.

WHAT IS STILL NOT ESTABLISHED
------------------------------
catchup-on reaches 0.770 while an offline decoder on the same states reads
0.876. The gap is smaller but real, so the readout is still leaving something
on the table even against a frozen column.

And this is a *schedule* result, not a demonstration that the mechanism works
online. A system that has to stop learning before its readout can use what it
learned has not solved continual learning; it has deferred it. That is the
next thing to fix, not to report around.

NEXT

2. Re-run the offline probe on the catchup condition, to see whether the
   remaining 0.770-vs-0.876 gap is extraction or something else.
