Editing this file starts a 20-seed run. It exists so that writing a *result*
into experiments/sweeps/ does not re-run the matrix that produced it.

run 1 -- sweep 028: does the binding gain survive a bigger column? Every number
  in this project comes from 96 neurons, and TODO item 1 says that if the gain
  vanishes at 1024 the mechanism is a small-column artefact and everything
  downstream changes. Ten conditions: binding on/off at 48/96/192/384, plus the
  smallest column run at the largest column's samples-per-feature.

  That last pair is the control and the sweep turns on it. probe.logistic uses
  l2=1e-4, essentially unregularised, so a bigger column starves the decoder --
  and binding *halves effective rank* (sweep 020), which is exactly the property
  that decides whether a decoder overfits. So a rising gain could be the
  representation improving, or binding making the decoder's job easier at high
  dimension, and those grow apart with N by construction. Running 48 neurons at
  384's samples-per-feature separates them.

  A one-seed pilot shows the gain rising steeply (+0.027, +0.040, +0.200 at
  48/96/192). The prediction bets on a flat gain instead, from mechanism: fan-in
  is fixed at 8x16 at every size, so each neuron's computation is identical and
  only N varies. Prediction in experiments/sweeps/binding-028-capacity.txt.
